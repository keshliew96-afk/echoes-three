## Direct comparison against docs/reference/pass-the-fear.png

**DENSITY.** The reference floor is hand-painted brick with cracks, moss and old scorch, and it carries roughly thirty simultaneous fireballs, each its own white core + orange glow + flame trail, plus lingering ground fire. Echoes now beats it on measured floor variation (FLAT 1.22-1.85% against the reference's 16.68%, every sampled box 0.00-4.81%) and matches it on edge dressing (15-16 prop types per scene, props on all four edges in every run frame, a dressed boss arena). Where Echoes is still clearly thinner: **projectile count and projectile layering** — 2 to 6 bolts on screen, each a white pill plus glow with no trail and no particles — and **floor memory**: the reference accumulates scorch and blood, Echoes accumulates nothing, which I proved with a locked camera (a boar dies inside box (1000,340,280,200); the same box 6 s later is 0.00% changed).

**COHESION.** The reference runs one lighting story: warm fire action funnelled against a cool violet void, cool 76.4%, 27% of pixels in the darkest bucket. Camp matches it almost exactly (cool 74.4 / warm 23.5 vs 76.4 / 22.5, 16/16 buckets, vignette 23-42 against a 104 centre) and the boss frame gets close (cool 25.0%, >200 1.287% vs 1.427%, the widest value range of the set). Combat is the outlier: its surround is dark olive-green rather than cool (foliage 52.0% / cool 8.0%), a legitimate Reference-C reading but not the reference's palette. Reserved-band discipline is now genuinely clean across all four frames — violet 93.5% inside the camp portal, enemy spines out of the violet band entirely, grass out of the heal band entirely, danger 26-125 px on both no-threat frames.

**POLISH.** The reference HUD is ornate gold metal: hex HP gem, boss medallion and name plate, illustrated weapon cards with ammo counts, drawn currency icons. Echoes' HUD is charcoal-and-amber rather than gold, but it now carries every element the bar names and some the reference does not: drawn portrait busts with class rims, HP bars and a critical-state numeral; drawn skill icons with true sweeping cooldown radials; dashed empty sockets; a boss plate with a stag medallion, arrowhead bar caps and phase pips; a shop shelf with gold corner brackets, rarity-bordered cards and glittering coin plaques. This is the single biggest change since round 1, and it is at the bar.

**BLUNT ANSWER.** Camp, shop and boss read as finished commercial frames — a player handed a screenshot of any of them would call it a shipped game. Combat reads as a finished game frame with one broken system: 350 ms before an enemy attack lands, the entire screen contains 198 red-orange pixels and the incoming hit is three ten-pixel beads next to the party. That is a fight you cannot play by looking at it. Second, nothing you kill leaves a mark, so the arena never records what happened in it. Third, the Hollow Stag is still an antlered obelisk rather than a stag — the one silhouette in the game that should be unmistakable is the one that is not.
