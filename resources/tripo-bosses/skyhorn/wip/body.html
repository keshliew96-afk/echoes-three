Skyhorn from Tripo, rigged · version 1

Your export of the Still Spire's warden, the great ibex whose curled horns were cast in bell bronze, with a skeleton and 12 moves added by hand. It walks on four locked hooves, rears and paws before the Horn Rush, slams into the wall at the end of the lane and stands dazed, clashes its horns for the Bell Toll and bounds into the air for the Sky Leap. Its timings are the game's. Pick a move, drag to turn it, and judge the weight and the hit. Nothing in the game has changed.

It came through whole, in one piece: the white coat with the grey saddle and mane, the long beard, the two bronze horns curled back over the body with whole tips, the bronze bell on a strap round the neck, the amber eyes and four slender legs with dark hooves. It stands exactly on the floor and its face points straight ahead (the eyes mirror each other within 1 mm), so nothing was corrected. Your design sheet shows a violet vein along each horn; the export has none, so the horns' violet sheen on this page is a glow over the whole horn.

Open choices

  1. The rear before the rush. It rears and paws while the lane fills (mine; the game only says it paws the floor). It could stand and paw only, which reads less dramatic but keeps it low.
  2. The dazed motes. Three pale motes circle its head while it is dazed, so the punish window reads at a glance. They are a page stand-in; the game shows no mark for dazed today, so keeping them would need the freeze lifted.
  3. The enrage. The rear, the beating hooves and the horns thrown up are mine.
  4. The turn. Mine, 90 degrees a second; the game turns it at once.
  5. The death. On its side with the legs loose. It could fold down onto its belly with its legs under it instead, like the Stag.
  6. The horns' violet vein. The design sheet has one; the export does not. It could be painted onto the texture, or you can re-export.
  7. The chained rush. Enraged, a rush that misses the wall chains straight into a second one. This page shows a single rush into the wall.

Rough so far

  • The wall at the end of the lane is a low, see-through stand-in block, so it does not hide the dazed pose.
  • The game backs it off to make room when the hero stands at its horns; the page does not show that step.
  • The hero is a grey stand-in that is thrown aside but does not dodge.
  • No ink outline and no flat game colours yet, the same as the hero models.
  • The effects are stand-ins drawn for this page in the game's colours: Ember for the floor warnings, Violet for its power, grey stone for the shards. They are kept dim on purpose.
