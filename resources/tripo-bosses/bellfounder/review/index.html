Bellfounder from Tripo, rigged · version 1

Your export of the Kiln's founder: a walking brick kiln with a bronze bell for a helmet, an iron door on its chest and slag-dark arms. It now carries the crucible you made, has a skeleton and 12 moves made by hand. Its hands stay on the crucible's handles in every move. It tips the crucible down a lane for the Molten Pour and heaves it overhead to throw a fresh bell for the Bell Drop. For the Firing it sets the crucible down, throws its door open and lets the heat out, then stands grey and quenched. The timings come from the game. Pick a move and drag to turn the view, then judge the weight and the hit. Nothing in the game has changed.

Model as you sent it

four views

The body came through in one piece, and the crucible as a second file. It has the bronze bell helmet with violet eye slits and a loop on top, the brick body with bright mortar lines, the arched iron door with glowing bars, slag-dark arms with open palms, brick legs with grey stone boots, and a small bell on a chain at each hip. Its face points straight ahead, so nothing was corrected. Two things were joined that a skeleton needs apart. The upper arms are fused to the body's sides, and each hip bell is fused to the outside of its thigh. I cut both free and capped the cuts. The door is cut free too, so it swings on its hinges. The crucible is scaled to sit in its hands at belly height, with a molten surface added inside.

Open choices

  1. The brick colour. Your export's brick is a bright orange red. The game's Kiln brick is darker and smokier. It could stay as it is, or be toned down to match.
  2. The arms. They were fused to its sides, so I cut them free at the shoulder. When an arm lifts high, a grey cap shows where it was cut. A re-export with the arms clear of the body would hide that.
  3. The door. One door on hinges at its left. The game's stand-in has two leaves that part.
  4. The crucible's size. I scaled it to 0.76 u wide so it fits the hands and leaves the door in view. It could be bigger.
  5. The crucible's colour. It takes one iron colour from the body's texture. Its own texture could be carried over instead.
  6. The Casting pose. Mine. The game only shows a broken mould.
  7. The death. It sits and slumps with the crucible tipped beside it. Another way is for it to fall forward onto the crucible.
  8. The turn. Mine, at 70 degrees a second. The game turns it at once.

Rough so far

  • The flying bell, the molten stream and the mould are drawn by the page. They are not in the GLB.
  • The hero is a grey stand-in that takes the hit but does not dodge.
  • No ink outline and no flat game colours yet, the same as the hero models.
  • The effects are stand-ins drawn for this page in the game's colours: Ember for the floor warnings, Kiln Gold for the heat and the bronze, and Violet for its eyes. They are kept dim on purpose.
