Hourwarden from Tripo, rigged · version 1

Your export of the Spire's last warden: a stone and bronze clock tower on a bed of gears, with a pagoda cap, violet eyes under the hood, a pale clock face on its chest, a pendulum in an arched window front and back, and two great fists that each hold a clock weight on a chain. It now has a skeleton, a swinging pendulum, a clock hand that turns, swinging chains and 12 moves made by hand. It glides on its gear bed. For the Pendulum it swings its own pendulum hard and a ghost of the swing runs down the lane. For the Hand Sweep it swings both weights across its front, and for the Toll of the Hours it strikes its own clock face once for each hour while clock weights fall onto the rings, then runs down and stands still before it winds up again. The timings come from the game. Pick a move and drag to turn the view, then judge the weight and the hit. Nothing in the game has changed.

It came through in one piece. The arms, the chains and the weights are clear of the body, so they were cut free cleanly. The pendulums and the clock hand were carved into the case as relief, so I cut each one free along its groove and filled the hole behind it with the dark of the window and the pale of the dial. The gears are carved into the bed the same way. Its face points straight ahead, so nothing was corrected.

Open choices

  1. The gears. They are carved into the bed, so they cannot turn. A re-export with the gears as separate parts, or gears built for it, would let them roll as it glides.
  2. The pendulum and the hand. They were carved in, so I cut them free. A re-export with them standing clear would give a cleaner edge.
  3. The Pendulum swing. Its own pendulum swings out of sight inside the case, so the big swing down the lane is a ghost drawn by the page.
  4. Weights, not blades. The game's stand-in has blade hands. Your export holds clock weights, so the Hand Sweep swings the weights.
  5. Striking its own dial. Mine. The game's stand-in strikes a bell for the Toll. It could strike the floor or ring a bell of its own instead.
  6. The hours. The page strikes 3. The game strikes 4 from phase two and 6 when enraged; those would be the same strike repeated.
  7. The enrage, the death, the turn and the spawn rise. Mine. It falls on its back with the dial up when it dies.
  8. No wind key. Your sheet has none, so the wind-up after the Toll is a shudder rather than a key turning.

Rough so far

  • The ghost pendulum, the falling weights and the clock hand's turning are drawn by the page. They are not in the GLB.
  • The hero is a grey stand-in that takes the hit but does not dodge.
  • No ink outline and no flat game colours yet, the same as the hero models.
  • The effects are stand-ins drawn for this page in the game's colours: Ember for the floor warnings, Violet for the song in its eyes, dial and pendulum, and gold for the sparks. They are kept dim on purpose.
