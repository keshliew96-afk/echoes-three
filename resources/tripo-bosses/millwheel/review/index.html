Millwheel from Tripo, rigged · version 5

Your export of the second Level II boss, rigged as what it is: a wheel with no legs. It rolls on the ends of its twelve pegs, and the peg on the floor is its foot: it stays where it is while the wheel pivots over it, then the next peg takes the weight. The two iron brackets on the axle hang free and swing like pendulums with every lurch. Its timings are the game's. Pick a move, drag to turn it, and judge the weight and the hit. Nothing in the game has changed.

Changed 13 Nov, your ask: Cog Shards is gone. In its place is the Plank Spin: the wheel jumps, lies down flat and spins, its twelve planks fly off one by one, each plank spins where it lands, and the planks fade away while the wheel grows new ones and stands up.

Changed 14 Nov, your ask: the wheel no longer grows new planks. Each socket stays empty while its plank spins on the floor. When the plank's 4 s are up, an Ember path lights from the plank to its socket, and the plank flies back down it, hitting anyone on the path, and snaps home. The wheel lies flat until all twelve are back, then stands up.

Changed 14 Nov, version 5, your answer: each wall hit now shakes loose 1 to 3 planks at random, the ones that struck the wall. If no plank is left on the wheel, a wall hit shakes none loose. A new plank lands clear of any already lying there.

Version 4, your ask: when the Crosscut hits the wall, the planks that struck it shake loose. Each falls off on its own, tumbling, bounces once, lies flat on an Ember mark that shows where it will come to rest, and spins there just like a Plank Spin plank: 4 s in its Violet ring, then the Ember path home, the flight back that hurts, and the snap into its socket. The wheel fights on with the gaps; it drops onto its rim where a socket is empty.

Idle or Roll sets what it does between attacks, and Going turns it on the spot. An Action plays over it and repeats until you press it again. Hurt plays on top of anything. Death holds it down: press it again and it spawns back in. A wheel can only change what it is doing while a peg is flat on the floor, so from a roll a move waits for the next peg (0.28 s at most), and Idle makes it rock to a stop. The grey slab is a stand-in for the room's far wall, drawn see-through so it does not hide the slam.

Open choices

  • Moves start on a peg. An attack, a hit or a death waits until a peg is flat on the floor: up to 0.28 s out of a roll, and up to 0.6 s if you catch the idle in its restless roll. That is the price of a peg that never slides. Say if a hit should land at once and let the wheel jump.
  • The stop. Rocking to rest out of a roll is mine. Say if it should stop dead as the game does.
  • Heavy hit distance. 0.67 u, one whole peg, so it ends standing on a peg. The game's 0.3 u would leave it between two.
  • Plank Spin numbers. You gave the move; the numbers are mine: all twelve planks fly, each lands at a random distance 2.0 to 5.4 u out, spins for 4 s in a 0.55 u ring, then flies back to its socket. Say if fewer should fly, if they should go further, or stay longer. It is a new mechanic, so it reaches the game only after the freeze.
  • The spinning wheel itself. It does not hurt anyone while it lies spinning; only the planks do. Say if standing beside it should hurt too.
  • The path back. Mine: the path lights 0.5 s before the plank lifts, is 0.6 u wide, and the plank takes 0.42 s to fly home. The wheel lies flat for about 4.4 s waiting for its planks, the party's window to hit it. Say if the warning should be longer, the hit softer, or the wait shorter (a shorter spin on the floor brings them home sooner).
  • The Crosscut's loose planks. Your rule: every wall hit shakes loose 1 to 3 planks at random, and none once no plank is left on the wheel. Where they land is mine: one to each side of the wheel and the third further out and back on one side, moved along if a plank already lies there. It is a new mechanic, so it reaches the game only after the freeze.
  • The cut's length. 5.39 u, eight whole pegs. In the game it runs to whatever wall is there; the rig can run any whole number of pegs.
  • The cog ring. It is one piece with the rim, so it glows when the wheel winds up and does not spin by itself. A spinning ring would need it as its own Tripo piece.
  • What glows. The game's wheel glows along its spokes. Here the cog ring and the hub lights glow, because that is what your model has.
  • How it lies dead. On its side, propped on the axle end and the rim, brackets on the floor.
  • Turn speed. 120° a second is my number, the same as the wolf and the heron.

What is rough

  • Pressing a second Action while one is playing cuts straight to it, and the wheel can jump up to half a peg at that instant.
  • A flying plank is the wheel's own plank end, drawn 1.8 times longer, as if the part inside the rim came out with it.
  • The planks leave in the same shuffled order every time; only where they land changes. They fly straight to their marks and do not bounce. The Crosscut's loose planks do bounce, once; each fall is worked out the moment it hits the wall, so its mark is exact.
  • With planks out, the wheel still rolls; at an empty socket it sinks onto its rim, by about 0.24 u with three gone in a row, more with more gone, and rises again on the next plank. It does not lurch or slow there.
  • Lying flat it stands on the end of its axle, so the rim rides 0.28 u off the floor with one bracket under it.
  • The pegs do not flex or chip, and the moss does not move.
  • The brackets swing as stiff pieces.
  • The far wall and the hero are grey stand-ins. The hero is knocked aside by the cut and stands still while the wheel is dizzy.
  • No ink outline and no flat game colours yet, the same as the hero models.
  • The effects are stand-ins drawn for this page in the game's colours: Ember for the floor warnings, Violet for its power, pale dust and sparks for wood and iron on stone. They are kept dim on purpose.
