Her moves

The rule for all of it: she is a bell-ringer. Every blow is a strike on a bell: a clean wind, one stroke, and the mallet bounces off and is left to ring. She is round and very short in the arm, so the blow comes from her whole body rocking, and her own bell answers.

Idle. Patient and a little proud: she breathes in her belly, rocks from boot to boot, glances up at her bell, and her antennae feel the air one at a time.

Walk and run. The walk is a round waddle with the bell swinging out of time with her steps. The run is a hurrying trot, head down, with the wing cases lifted off her back.

Mallet combo, three hits in 1.6 seconds, a bigger bell each time. She never hits the enemy: she hits a bell, and the note does the damage. A copy of her bell fades in where each stroke will land. Hit 1 (0.22 s): a small bell ahead, tapped. Hit 2 (0.56 s): a middle bell at her right front, struck backhand so it rocks sideways. Hit 3 (1.02 s): a great bell fades in overhead, drops to the floor in front of her as she rises, and she tolls it with her whole weight; a ring runs out along the floor and the bell on her back answers. Each bell fades out after it rings.

Casts. Clapper (0.36 s): a bell fades in ahead and one long stroke sends it flying forward, ringing as it goes; this covers clapper, knell and tuning fork. Peal (0.50 s): she lifts the mallet like a conductor and rocks side to side; on each of the three beats a small bell fades in around her and rings, and any bell she has hung rings with them; for peal, hum, vesper chime and carillon. Hang a bell (0.40 s): a bell fades in and settles on the floor ahead as she sets it down, she gives it one soft tap, and it stays for about three seconds; for hang bell and great bell. Bell swing (0.44 s): a big bell fades in at her right and her stroke swings it across her front in an arc to her left. Wing hop: she crouches, the wing cases snap open, and she springs up and lands with a bounce.

Dash and downed. In the dash her wing cases open and she buzzes forward with her boots tucked. When downed she sits down hard with her legs out and her head hung, and scoots along on her seat; lying flat would have put the pole and the bell through the floor.

Effects. The summoned bells are copies of the bell on her back (476 triangles each, up to three at once in the peal), fading in over about a tenth of a second and out over a fifth. Bell Brass for a struck note and Verdigris for her own bell answering, the Bellwright's two colours in the game, and Old Bronze for the dust. Each struck bell gives one thin ring that opens and fades. All of it is dim and switches off with Hit feel, which leaves the bare moves.

What works

  • The bell really swings from its hook, and it stays clear of her head and her cap in every move.
  • The four casts and the wing hop cover her 11 skills in the game.
  • The wing cases, the antennae and the bell all trail her body and settle after she stops.
  • The mallet stays in her paw and clear of her face, belly and cape.

What is rough

  • Her arms are very short (about 16 cm of reach on a body 40 cm wide), so the strokes are short and the summoned bells have to come in close to her.
  • The summoned bells are placed by the page at fixed spots, not by the mallet: at full speed the mallet should meet each bell within a few centimetres, but I only saw that in stills. They are a new mechanic compared with the game today, where only hang bell places a bell.
  • The wing cases are stiff: each half turns as one piece from the shoulder, so they open but do not bend.
  • The pole and the pack frame do not flex, and the pole stands right behind her cap, as it came: when she tips her head far back the brim can touch the pole.
  • She has no knees or elbows to speak of, her fingers don't close round the mallet, and the neckerchief is stiff.
  • Her feet can slide while she walks, the moves play in place (the wing hop does not carry her forward), and she has no ink outline or flat game colours yet.
