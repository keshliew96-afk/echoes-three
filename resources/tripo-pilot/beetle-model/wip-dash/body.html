Her moves

The rule for all of it: she is a bell-ringer. Every blow is a strike on a bell: a clean wind, one stroke, and the mallet bounces off and is left to ring. She is round and very short in the arm, so the blow comes from her whole body rocking, and her own bell answers.

Idle. Patient and a little proud: she breathes in her belly, rocks from boot to boot, glances up at her bell, and her antennae feel the air one at a time.

Walk and run. The walk is a round waddle with the bell swinging out of time with her steps. The run is a hurrying trot, head down, with the wing cases lifted off her back.

Mallet combo, six strokes in 1.7 seconds. Hit 1 (0.22 s): a small faded bell appears at the mallet and her tap shoots it forward. When it reaches its range (1.3 m on this page, since the page has no enemy to hit) it floats up, grows to 2.5 times its size while it turns from faded to solid, then smashes down: floor rings, dust and a shake mark the area it would damage. Hit 2 (0.56 s): the same from a backhand, shot out to her right front, so the two bells land apart. Hit 3 (from 0.70 s): a great bell fades in lying on its side in front of her, mouth forward, as in your picture. She drums its rim three times fast (0.90, 1.00, 1.10 s), each sending one sound wave out along the floor in a fan, then one big stroke (1.22 s) sends three waves, and the bell on her back answers. The great bell fades as she recovers.

Casts. Clapper (0.36 s): a bell fades in ahead and one long stroke sends it flying forward, ringing as it goes; this covers clapper, knell and tuning fork. Peal (0.50 s): she lifts the mallet like a conductor and rocks side to side; on each of the three beats a small bell fades in around her and rings, and any bell she has hung rings with them; for peal, hum, vesper chime and carillon. Hang a bell (0.40 s): a bell fades in and settles on the floor ahead as she sets it down, she gives it one soft tap, and it stays for about three seconds; for hang bell and great bell. Bell swing (0.44 s): a big bell fades in at her right and her stroke swings it across her front in an arc to her left. Wing hop: she crouches, the wing cases snap open, and she springs up and lands with a bounce.

Dash: the bell (13 November). She throws the mallet straight up and calls (0.10 s), looking up as a great bell drops out of the air over her; she ducks as it lands round her (0.36 s) with a ring on the floor, dust and a shake. The bell is the bronze bell model you sent on 13 November (4,840 triangles), 1.68 u tall and 0.8 u from its middle to its rim, sized so that her cape and her pole are inside it. From the moment it lands she cannot be hit and walks where you steer with Going, at one and a half times her walking speed; the bell shuffles with her steps and a label counts the time down. After 2.5 s it blinks, lifts and fades. Tap attack or any cast lifts it at once and plays; pressing Dash again lifts it too. It is the longest dash of the seven heroes and the slowest. This is new compared with the game today and needs your go and the freeze lifted before it enters it.

Dash and downed. Walk, idle and the strafes are bigger than in Version 2. When downed she sits down hard with her legs out and her head hung, and scoots along on her seat; lying flat would have put the pole and the bell through the floor.

Effects. The summoned bells are copies of the bell on her back (476 triangles each, at most four on screen at once, in the combo), her own bell never leaves her pack. A shot bell flies at about 40% strength and is fully solid only for the smash. The sound waves are thin arcs on the floor that open in a fan and fade. Bell Brass for a struck note and Verdigris for her own bell answering, the Bellwright's two colours in the game, and Old Bronze for the dust. Each struck bell gives one thin ring that opens and fades. All of it is dim and switches off with Hit feel, which leaves the bare moves.

Audit and review, 12 November

How it was done. Version 2 only measured the mallet's middle line against rough body centres. For this version every vertex of the model was placed in every frame of all 25 moves (60 a second), and the true gap between each pair of parts was taken: mallet ball and shaft against head, trunk, arms, legs, cape, antennae, pole and bell; the bell against pole, head and antennae; the head against the pole; arms and legs against the cape; cape and boots against the floor. Then smoothness was scored per bone (jerks, dead stops, loop pops), and every move was looked at as still frames from the front, side, top and the game camera. The cloud machine cannot play it at full speed, so timing, weight and the feel of the drumming are yours to judge.

Five faults the audit found, all fixed. 1. The cap brim cut 2 mm short of the pole and visibly into it when she tipped her head back; the pole now leans with her head and the bell hangs true. 2. The boots sank 1.2 cm into the floor in the walk; now 0.5 cm at most. 3. The mallet grazed her face on the way down in Hang a bell; it now goes round. 4. The bell swung past level in Hurt and the fall; it is now capped. 5. The summoned bells stood up to 9 cm from where the mallet actually lands; each is now placed from the measured mallet position, and the bell swing's contact moved to 0.42 s.

What works

  • The bell really swings from its hook, and it stays 13 cm or more clear of her head in every move.
  • The four casts and the wing hop cover her 11 skills in the game.
  • The bell shot reads clearly from the game camera: faded in flight, solid for the smash.
  • The wing cases, the antennae and the bell all trail her body and settle after she stops.
  • The mallet stays in her paw and clear of her face, belly and cape.

What is rough

  • The dash bell is your bell model as it came, a redder bronze than the small bells she carries. The bells of her combo and casts are still copies of her pole bell.
  • She is not safe in the 0.36 s before the bell lands. The other six cannot be hit from the first frame of their dash. Say if you want her safe from the first frame too. The 2.5 s and the speed are first numbers.
  • The bell shots land at a fixed range because the page has no enemies. In the game they would stop at the first foe. Range, area and damage are yours to set, and all of this is a new mechanic that needs your go before it enters the game.
  • Her arms reach only about 16 cm, so the great bell has to lie very close to her and the drumming strokes are short. I only saw the drumming as stills.
  • The mallet passes 3 cm from her right antenna in the peal.
  • The wing cases are stiff: each half turns as one piece, so they open but do not bend. The second tuning fork is still on the back of the cape.
  • She has no knees or elbows to speak of, her fingers do not close round the mallet, and the neckerchief is stiff.
  • Her feet can slide while she walks, the moves play in place, and she has no ink outline or flat game colours yet.
