Her moves

The rule for all of it: poised, precise and springy. She stands side-on behind the bow with the arrow's line running past the front of her chest. The bow is held close while she nocks and pushed out as she draws, and the loose is the accent: the string snaps, the bow kicks, the draw paw flies back, and her long ears whip after it.

Idle, now 6 seconds long. At ease but listening: the bow hangs in her left paw, her weight shifts from foot to foot, and each ear turns on its own to a sound. She tugs the quiver strap where it meets her belt (0.7 s) and glances to her left (1.7 s). Then she rises on her toes to look the other way, tall as a hare in long grass, both ears straight up and her free paw at her chest (3.9 s), with a quick second look and one ear turning back (4.3 s). She settles and thumps her back foot twice (5.25 and 5.44 s); the ears shiver, the tail flicks and a little dust comes off the foot.

Walk and run. The walk is a light, springy step up on her toes with the bow carried still; her shoulders now roll with the step, the tail bobs and the ear tips nod a beat behind the ears. Backing off, she keeps her eyes on what she backs away from, the free paw out for balance and the ears tipped forward. Stepping sideways, her chest and head lead a little and the long ears trail away from the way she goes, the tips most. The run is a bounding run, far forward, with a bigger bound than before, the spine pumping, the tail bouncing and the ears laid back and streaming.

Bow combo, three shots in 1.5 seconds. Shot 1 (0.22 s) is a snap shot off a half draw. Shot 2 (0.54 s) follows at once: her paw is already back at the quiver while the first arrow flies. Shot 3 (1.00 s) is the finisher: she lays the bow over flat, takes three arrows at once, sinks behind a full draw and holds a beat, then looses all three in a spread, and the recoil hops her back half a step.

Casts. Power shot (0.62 s): the bow comes up high, she draws slowly as it lowers onto the target, holds, and looses with a jade streak; this covers piercing shot, pinning arrow and the charged shots. Sky volley (0.52 s): she leans back, aims high and looses; the arrows come down ahead of her a moment later; for volley and rain of arrows. Trap (0.42 s): she drops low and presses it onto the floor ahead with her free paw, the bow held back out of the way; for barbed trap and the marks. Feather fan (0.36 s): the bow is laid over flat and a fan of five leaves it level. It now looks close to the combo's last shot; say if you want it changed. Vault shot (0.36 s): she springs straight back off both feet and shoots at the top of the hop.

Dash: a somersault and three homing arrows (13 November). She crouches deep (0.11 s) and pushes off hard (0.15 s). On the way up she turns one tight forward somersault (0.20 to 0.48 s), and while she is upside down her free paw takes three arrows from the quiver. She opens out at the top of the jump, 0.75 u up, side-on with the bow laid flat and all three arrows on the string, draws (0.57 s) and looses (0.60 s). The three arrows leave in a spread and bend in on the nearest foe; on this page a practice post stands for it and counts the hits. She lands at 0.75 s, sinks right down and the floor shakes. The jump covers 2.6 u and she cannot be hit until she lands. This is new compared with the game today and needs your go and the freeze lifted before it enters it.

Small things added to every shot (13 November). The back heel lifts as she draws and the front toes lift as the bow kicks. Her head dips after each arrow and the tail jerks. An upright bow rocks its top toward the target as the string snaps. In the power shot the held draw trembles, most in her ears, and the recoil walks her back foot back before she settles. In the sky volley she comes up on her toes as she looses, her head follows the arrows up with the ears laid back, then comes down to the target. Setting the trap, she pats it down twice, glancing up one way and then the other with one ear turned each time, tail up, and springs back to her feet. After the feather fan and the vault shot the ears and tail carry on a beat after she stops, and her heels dig in on the vault's landing.

Hurt, wave and downed, redone for her (13 November). Hurt (0.6 s): a startled hare. The blow lifts her off both feet with her ears left behind, the bow pulled in and the free paw up at her face (0.06 s); she lands back and low with the ears whipping back (0.17 s); then the ears come up one after the other and she shakes it off with a flick of the tail. Wave (2.2 s): a quick dip, then up on her toes with the free paw high over her head, waving it wide; she bounces with each wave, her head tips to the paw, one ear leans away to make room and the other bends out at the tip, the tail wags; she ends with a small bob of a bow, ears dipping forward. Downed: the blow throws her head and ears back, her legs give, she folds forward and bounces once, and the ears flop on over her face a beat late. Lying, she breathes slowly, one ear still listens and a back foot twitches. Getting up, her head lifts first and one ear stands, she gathers her legs, springs up onto her toes and shakes her head so both ears flap after it.

Effects. Feather Bone for the arrows and Wind Jade for the wind on a strong shot, the Archer's two colours in the game. A pale streak for each arrow, a few motes off the string, a little dust under fast feet, a jade ring where the trap sits. All of them are dim and switch off with Hit feel. The two snap shots barely freeze and do not shake the camera; the full draw and the power shot do.

What works

  • The string really bends and the arrow really sits on it: the draw paw is solved onto the string in every frame, wherever the bow is.
  • The four casts and the vault shot cover her 11 skills in the game.
  • Ears and tail trail the body and settle after she stops; the head and the draw arm follow the trunk a few hundredths of a second late.
  • Her feet stay on the floor when she sinks into the full draw or drops to set the trap.

What is rough

  • The arrow is thick, as it came. It reads at game zoom, but a thinner arrow would look more like the ones in her quiver.
  • The arrows in the quiver are part of her body, so none leaves the quiver when she reaches for one: her paw goes to the quiver and the arrow appears on the string.
  • Her draw is short (about 15 cm on a 40 cm bow), because her arms are short and her chest is deep. A longer draw would put her paw inside her chest.
  • She has no knees, so sinking low is a lean and a drop of the hips. Her fingers don't close round the bow or the string, and the skirt is one stiff piece.
  • Her feet can slide while she walks, the moves play in place (the vault does not carry her back), and she has no ink outline or flat game colours yet.
  • The downed moves, Hurt and Wave use the fox's timing with her ears added; they were not designed for her from scratch.
