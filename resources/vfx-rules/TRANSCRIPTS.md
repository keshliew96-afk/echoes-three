## 1. Lightning VFX in 7 Steps

Here's how to make a lightning effect in your game in seven steps. Step 1, draw a squiggle,
not white. That comes later. Step 2, exaggerate the action. Push the corners out and make
every turn sharper. Step 3, give the line some shape: thick at the bends, thin in between,
organic or sharp, your call. Step 4, on a layer underneath, paint a broad, soft glow: loose,
atmospheric, low opacity. Step 5, closer to the center, a narrower glow in a brighter color.
Step 6, accents: little arcs and sparks around the bolt. Taper every stroke thick to thin.
Step 7, add the zap: a sharp, bright, white core for contrast. And now it's electric.
Now make it move. Draw three or four versions of the bolt and swap them every couple of
frames. Start with the thickest, brightest frame and a quick flash. Then let it thin out,
break apart and fade. The whole thing lasts about a quarter of a second. Loop it or trigger
it on hit, and that's your lightning.

## 2. How to make a punch juicy

This hit works, but it feels like nothing. Here's how to make it punchy in five steps.
Step 1, flash. Turn the enemy white for just a few frames. Step 2, add sound. A sharp whack
right on the impact frame. Step 3, particles. Blast them out in the direction of the hit.
Step 4, knockback. Push the enemy away and bounce the player back a bit (on-screen caption:
"BOUNCE THE PLAYER BACK A BIT!"; both speech passes heard "back of it"). Step 5, shake and
freeze. Tiny screen shake on impact and freeze the game for a few frames. Out of health?
Pop it like a balloon. Save this for later.

## 6. The Disney Secret to Juicy, Satisfying Animation

Same fireball: top one rules off, bottom one rules on. You'd think the difference is more
particles and more glow. It's not the particles. It's not the shaders. It's rules Disney
animators wrote in the 1930s. Step 1, anticipation. The hero leans back while the fire
gathers, then throws. Step 2, squash and stretch. The fireball stretches in flight and the
enemy squashes on the hit. But here's the better way to make it feel alive: step 3, follow
through. The hero's body settles after the throw, the trail lags behind, and the burst flies
on. But none of this lands unless you nail step 4, timing. Freeze the game for a few frames
on the hit. And here's the best part. Step 5, arcs. Nothing flies straight. Not the sparks,
not the damage numbers. 90 years old, still works. Send this to your friends.

## 9. 8 steps to a juicy ice attack
