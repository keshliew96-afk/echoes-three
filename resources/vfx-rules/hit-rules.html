The ten laws

What all nine videos teach together. Every rule below serves one of these.

  1. One impact frame.

    On the hit's own frame these start together: the flash, the thump, the freeze, the first shake kick and the number. The push and the attacker's recoil start there too, unless a freeze holds them. Then they launch on the first frame after the freeze (F1). On a big finish the first shake kick may trail by 1 to 2 frames (v9). Particles start on the same frame or one frame later: v2's shards one frame later, v3's streaks and v4's blood puff with the flash. Only the attacker's own swing layers start earlier: the whoosh with the wind-up and, in v8, the tip glint and the trail's build during the strike. After that, each layer decays on its own clock.

  2. The target must react.

    "If the target doesn't react, you can't tell you hit it." The target flashes white, squashes, is pushed away along the hit and settles. Target reaction matters more than attacker flourish.

  3. Every press answers on the same frame.

    A flash at the hand or muzzle, a weapon kick and a sound, all on the frame of the press.

  4. One direction.

    Particles leave the far side of the target, and the target flies the same way. A bullet hit also sprays chips and droplets back toward the shooter (v4). On a melee hit the first shake kick goes along the hit too (v2). The spell, lightning and ice videos kick in a random direction (v1, v3, v9; F3). The attacker recoils the other way.

  5. Anticipation, action, follow-through.

    Lean back and gather, strike fast, hold the contact, then settle with overshoot. Trails lag behind, and bursts fly on.

  6. Build from colour to white.

    The shape comes first, in a mid-value colour. Then a broad soft glow underneath, a narrower brighter glow, tapered accents, and the white core last. Every stroke tapers to a point. A swing trail is thick at the blade and thin toward the swing's start (L3, T4).

  7. Strongest first, then decay.

    The first drawing of the impact is the biggest and brightest. Two things build up to that beat instead. A travelling effect grows toward the target: v9's spikes grow about 5 times, and the decay starts at the hit. A swing trail grows with the blade over 1 to 2 frames and peaks on contact, never on spawn (T5). After that the effect thins, breaks apart and fades. Shocks last about 0.1 to 0.25 s. The things that tell the story (embers, a burn, marks, numbers) last 0.5 to 2 s.

  8. Nothing flies straight.

    Sparks, debris and damage numbers travel on arcs. The shot itself keeps its straight line, so the hit stays readable.

  9. The last hit is the biggest.

    The kill gets a longer freeze, a bigger flash, a throw or a pop, its own sound, and a mark that stays.

  10. Never the same twice.

    Vary size, angle, speed and pitch on every shot and sound. "Identical bullets look like a laser." "The same sound twice in a row feels fake."

The rules

How to read a rule

Every rule has an id, a tag, a layer and a status.

Tag: who carries it.

  • [M] Models and animation. Rig poses, the body's squash and stretch, anticipation, recoil, follow-through, death motion, and the named hand and tip points that effects anchor to (B8). The effect drawn at such a point is [V]. Every model standard slice (hero, boss, champion, foe) carries the [M] rules for the models it builds. The checklist is in §6.1.
  • [V] Hit feedback and VFX. Flashes, particles, trails, bursts, decals, numbers, camera, the post stack and sound. A VFX and hit feedback slice builds these once for the whole game. Its list is in §6.2.

Layer: what it touches.

  • R render only. The nine goldens stay identical.
  • A audio only. The goldens skip sound events, so they stay identical.
  • S sim. It moves the goldens. Only Kesh can name it (§6.3).
  • R/S a render part, plus a sim part that the sim already has or that only Kesh can name.
  • R/A a render part plus an audio part. The goldens stay identical.
  • A clash with a written ruling is the ⚠ status, whatever the layer. F2 and F3, for example, are R ⚠.

Status today. ✓ already does it · ~ partly · ✗ missing · ⚠ conflicts with a ruling.

Units.

  • The videos are 30 fps captures of a 60 fps game. One video frame is 33 ms, which is 2 Echoes ticks (the sim runs at 60 Hz).
  • bw means the target's body width. For a rank-and-file Echoes foe, 1 bw ≈ 0.65 u (its footprint radius is 0.3 to 0.35 u).
  • Eight videos use a fixed high three-quarter camera over a grid floor, very close to Echoes' own. Their targets are capsules (v7's is a chest). Their numbers in bw transfer directly.
  • v1 is the exception. It is a 2D side-on sketch: a dome-shaped blob 143 px wide and 105 px tall on a flat floor line. There is no 3D camera, only a tiny screen shake.
  • From v1, copy the timings, the flash curve, the shake, the layer and colour ramp, the hurt tint and the number motion.
  • Do not copy v1's geometry as it stands. Its bolt stands upright in screen space, about 2 blob widths tall. In Echoes, draw it as a camera-facing sprite or in screen space, or the camera foreshortens it. v1's bw is a blob that is wider than it is tall, so treat its sizes in bw as rough.

Terms.

  • Combo finisher means the third hit of a rig's 3-hit basic combo (Kesh's template, docs/MODEL_STANDARD.md). The rig counts it on the render clock.
  • It is not the Swordsman's Crescent Finisher and not the sim's combo stacks.
  • Only the Tank pilot has the combo today (src/render/critters/tankfacet.js). A rig without it has no finisher yet.

Pointers.

  • A bare § number from 8 up is a section of docs/BUILD_BRIEF.md. §9 #N is item N of its juice contract. A lower bare § number is this doc's own.
  • design-VFX is docs/gauntlet/design-VFX.md. A § number given with a file's name is that file's.
  • Ruling A5 (the party is never knocked back) is from BUILD_BRIEF's Appendix A. Rule A5 is this doc's sound rule (§2.11).

2.1 The impact frame

I1VFX

~render and audiov1, v2, v3, v4

  • The knockback starts there too when there is no freeze. Under a freeze it waits, then launches with the recoil on the first moving frame (F1).
  • The first particles start on that frame or the next one. v2's shards come 1 frame late. v3's streaks and v4's blood puff land with the flash.
  • Heavier debris can trail. In v4 the splash on the wall behind lands 2 frames after the hit, and the droplets back toward the shooter 3 frames after.
  • Today: every layer listens to the sim's hit event, so they all land on one frame. The exception is a damage number born within 0.16 s of another one close by on screen. It starts 0.05 s later for each such number, up to 0.15 s (src/render/numbers.js fan).

A cue still waiting after 100 ms (or 2.5 rendered frames, if longer) is dropped, not played late (src/audio/engine.js dispatchQueue). A 30 ms same-cue cooldown also merges a cleave's hits into one impact (src/audio/cues.js:161).
  • Fix: let the first impact of each tick bypass the budget. That is audio only.

2.2 The target reacts

R1VFX

Flash the target near-white with a hard cut.

~render onlyv2, v3, v4, v6, v8, v9
  • No fade-in. v2 and v3 keep a faint trace of the body's hue (#F0EAFF to #F2EEFC). v8's sword hits go pure white (#FDFDFD) under a big bloom.
  • Length: 2 to 3 frames (67 to 100 ms, 4 to 6 ticks). The freeze pauses the flash timer, so the total is the freeze plus 1 to 2 frames (5 to 6 frames in v2, and 4 to 5 in v8 on its 3-frame freeze).
  • A spell flash fades back over about 12 to 15 more frames, half-way by +6 (v3).
  • v6's fireball: near-white through the 3-frame hold, brightest on the 2 to 3 bloom frames after it, then mostly faded about 10 frames later. A thin gold rim (3 to 4 px) and a lilac tint fade with it.
  • So both spell videos fade over 10 to 15 frames (20 to 30 ticks).
  • A rapid-fire bullet hit flashes 1 frame, and the kill 2 to 3 (v4). A big finish flashes 4 (v9).
  • Today: emissive white at 1.0 for 3 ticks (50 ms), held through hitstop, so about 83 ms on a melee hit (src/core/constants.js:60-63, src/render/enemies/index.js hit handler). Shots and spells get 50 ms with no fade. Champions are scaled to 0.4, the Gale Herald to 0.25, and bosses peak at only 0.28 (BOSS_FLASH).
  • Safe route: a 4 to 6 tick hold, then a fade of about 24 to 30 ticks on spell hits, half-way back about 10 ticks after the flash starts (+6 in v3). That is render only.
R2Model

Squash the target on contact, then stretch it back.

✗render onlyv1, v3, v4, v6, v9
  • Frame 0 or 1: 10 to 14% wider and 12 to 13% shorter, with the base anchored.
  • Frames 2 to 3: a 6 to 7% stretch.
  • Settled by frame 5 to 8 (170 to 270 ms).
  • A rapid-fire bullet hit squashes less in height (v4): 12 to 17% wider and 5 to 8% shorter on the arrival frame. While hits keep landing it stays squashed, and each new hit does not restart the spring. It is back to shape within about 3 frames (100 ms) of the last hit.
  • A heavy spell hit squashes harder (v6): 1.18 to 1.3 wide by 0.85 to 0.91 tall, the top dropping and the base spreading. It holds through the freeze, springs to 1.05 to 1.1 tall and about 0.88 wide at +7 to 8 frames, and rests by +10 to 13. Count from the contact, with the hold included. That is a damped spring.
  • Without a hold (v6 step 2) it springs at +5 to 6 and rests by +7 to 8.
  • v8 does the opposite on a sword hit. On the first white frame the target stretches 14 to 17% taller, with the width and the base unchanged, and holds it through the freeze. It squashes only on landing. Five videos squash on contact and v8 stretches, so whether a blade hit squashes or stretches on contact stays an open choice.
  • The squash is oriented to the hit, and the ticks hold through the freeze like the flash.
  • Today: enemy rigs never squash on a hit, only in the death pop (the hit handler in src/render/enemies/index.js, and src/render/boss/index.js).
  • This is a per-rig scale envelope that works for all 42 foe kinds from the shared enemy layer (the Bestiary shows 41, because the broodling shares the brood's page, but it has its own rig).

R4Model

~render, plus a sim partv2, v4

  • Those rises are screen heights with the depth along the hit taken out. Echoes' 52° camera shows a world lift at about 0.6 of its height, so the hop needs about 0.4 u of world lift (about 0.6 u on a finisher).
  • A shot (v4): push it about 0.5 bw, with a lower hop of about 0.12 of its height. The hop peaks 2 to 3 frames after the hit and lands 5 to 6 frames after it (§3.3).
  • The timings below are the melee ones. They count from the launch: the impact frame, or the first frame after the freeze when there is one (F1). Under v2's 3-frame hold the push peaks at +4 to 5 and lands at +9 to 10, counted from the impact.
  • The push peaks at +2 frames. The body tilts back 7 to 12°, most at +1 to +2, and is upright again by +4 to +5. It tips 3 to 5° forward just before it lands. This tilt is the only lean in the hit reaction (R3 adds none).
  • In the air the body is about 10% taller and 12% narrower, clearest at +5 to +6. It lands at about +7 frames (0.23 s) with a 1-frame squash (+15% wide, −13% tall), recovers in 2 frames, then settles.

    • The sim slide is already the right distance: 0.34 u for a basic and 0.72 u for a skill (0.5 to 1.1 bw), over 10 ticks (src/core/constants.js:51-55).
    • The hop, tilt, air stretch and landing squash are missing.
  • Safe route: draw the hop, tilt and squash on the rig as a render overlay on the sim slide. Do not change the sim distances.
    • Scale the hop, the tilt and the air stretch to the distance the sim really moves the body: the hit's own kb (on the hit event) times the body's kbScale where it has one. A champion takes 0.1 to 0.15, the Barrow Ram and the Slag Golem 0.3, the Geode Brute 0.35, the Bone Knight 0.4 and an awake Gargoyle 0.5 (src/sim/enemies.js, src/sim/enemies/). The video's look is for a push of about 0.55 u.
    • No push, no hop. Bosses, a stone Gargoyle, a rooted Heart Bloom, a Bog Lamprey while it rises and lunges, and zero-knockback skills get only the squash. A burrowed Grave Mole cannot be hit at all. A surfaced mole and a beached lamprey are pushed like any foe.
    • A negative kb is a pull, so the body tilts toward the hitter.
  • The demo dummy springs back to its spot afterwards. That is a training-dummy habit. In Echoes the foe stays where the sim puts it.

2.3 Freeze and shake

F1VFX

Freeze on contact.

~render, plus a sim partv2, v4, v5, v6, v7, v8
  • Hold the attacker, the target and the trail for 3 frames (100 ms, 6 ticks), or 4 on some hits. Hold 5 frames (167 ms, 10 ticks) on a kill.
  • These lengths are measured on v2. v8 also holds 3 frames (once 2). v5's anime hit holds much longer, 9 to 10 frames (X1).
  • The hold starts on the impact frame. v7's chest is the exception: a reward holds at the top of its hop (S+4 to S+7), and its stars and light hold too (O3).
  • During the hold:
    • The particles keep moving.
    • The trail holds its sweep, but its texture keeps flickering (v8).
    • The flash timer pauses.
    • The knockback and the recoil wait. They release together on the first moving frame.
  • Today:
    • The sim hitstop is 2 ticks (33 ms) on a non-lethal melee arc and 3 ticks (50 ms) on any kill, with a budget of 4 ticks per 21-tick window (src/core/constants.js:32-37, src/sim/combat.js:53-68).
    • Shots, novas, zones and bites freeze only when they kill (the 3-tick kill pause).
    • No render layer listens to hitstop. Walk cycles, kit VFX, numbers and the kill pop keep running through it (src/render/enemies/index.js:319-321), so today's freeze reads as a position stall, not a held frame.
  • Safe route (render only): on each hitstop event, hold the rig clips of the attacker and the target, the slash ribbons' sweep (their noise keeps running) and the flare time, and let the particles run.
  • Kesh's call (sim): a longer hitstop (about 6 ticks on a hit and 10 on a kill), or a hitstop on shot and spell hits (§6.3 items 1 and 2). Both move the goldens and the §9 budget.
  • v6 freezes the whole game for 3 video frames (about 100 ms, 6 ticks) at 8 of its 9 hits, and for 2 at one.
    • Its sketch says "4 FRAMES". At 60 fps that would be only 67 ms, shorter than what it shows.
    • The hold is on the pose of greatest deformation: the fireball embedded, the target squashed and the number at its biggest.
    • Nothing in v6's hold moves, unlike v2's. The caster, the fireball and its trail, the squash and the number all hold. The burst and its streaks wait for the release.
    • In its side-by-side only the juicy game holds, so this is game time, not an edited pause.
    • The 2 to 3 frame white bloom is the release.
  • Note: the videos disagree on ranged hits. v3's spell, v9's ice attack and v4's shots have no freeze at all. v6 freezes its fireball. So a ranged hitstop stays an open choice.
    • v9 gets its weight from the flash, the overshoot, the shake and the jump in size instead.
  • v4's kill is not a world freeze, though its narration says "freeze for a beat". Only the camera and the gun stop, for 1 frame. The hero, the particles and the fading flash keep moving. The victim then holds upright and white for 2 to 3 frames before the throw (§3.3 step 7).

3.3 Juicy shots (v4; for hero bolts, and for the sci-fi machine foes' fire)

  1. The shot. On the press frame, a full-size, randomly turned white-hot starburst at the muzzle (0.6 to 0.75 bw). It lights the shooter, the floor and the target (+17%). On the next frame a smaller flame; on the third frame nothing. Continuous fire flickers at the fire rate.
  2. Bullets. Tracers, not dots. A hot head, and a tail about half the per-frame travel. Each shot gets its own angle (about ±6°) and speed (about ±20%). Wall hits throw a spark star of 5 to 10 streaks that lasts about 200 ms. Echoes: render jitter of size, brightness and height only. The angle and the speed stay the sim's (S4).
  3. The hit. React on the frame the bullet arrives (2 to 3 frames of flight), never later.
    • Flash the target near-white for 1 frame per bullet. Overlapping hits keep it white.
    • Squash it 12 to 17% wider and 5 to 8% shorter.
    • Push it about 0.5 bw along the shot with a small hop of about 0.12 of its height.
    • The shape recovers within about 100 ms. The push holds about 300 ms, then eases back over about 350 ms.
    • "Fast attack, slow release": the flash and squash snap in within 1 frame, while the displacement holds and returns slowly.
    • Echoes: the foe stays where the sim puts it, with no ease back (R4). A hero hit by machine fire flashes crimson and is never pushed (ruling A5).
  4. Recoil. On each shot:
    • Squash the shooter about +20% wide on the first shot. Steady fire deepens this to about +38% wide and −10% tall by the third shot. Push it back about 0.4 of its width.
    • Push the camera a few px against the fire direction, with a 3% zoom punch on the first shot of a burst.
    • After the last shot, the shape recovers in about 100 ms with a small stretch overshoot, and the position in about 0.45 s. The camera holds 0.25 s, then eases back over about 0.6 s.
    • Echoes: a rig-only shove of 0.08 to 0.12 u (B3). The camera moves only for your own hero's shots. For the Archer and the Healer even that waits for Kesh (C1, §6.3 item 7). Foe fire never moves the camera.
  5. The mess. Spray both ways from each hit, and leave it on the floor. Eject one casing per shot. Echoes: matter colours only, and no casings (D2).

  6. Sound. Each sound fires at its own event:

    • a crack at the muzzle on the shot frame;
    • a whiz while the bullet flies;
    • a thud on the frame the bullet arrives;
    • a ping when it reaches the wall.

    The on-screen words appear at exactly those events. Use few samples and nudge the pitch every play. In v4 the gun is silent until this step, and then it is about 30 dB louder in the low end. In the file the sound leads the flash in every burst, by 10 to 118 ms (median 55 ms, about 1.5 to 2 frames). That looks like an encode offset, so the file cannot prove frame-exact sync.

  7. The kill. The biggest beat:

    • The camera and gun stop for 1 frame. This is not a full world freeze: the hero and the victim keep moving.
    • The victim stays upright and white for 2 to 3 frames (an ordinary hit gets 1). Its HP bar goes on the kill frame.
    • A 7.4% camera punch-in, about 2.5 times a shot's punch, decaying over 7 to 10 frames.
    • The body topples about 90° in 4 frames (about 130 ms, easing in) along the shot, into the wall. It slides back about 0.5 bw off the wall, tinted darker, and rests about 0.67 s after it lands (0.87 s after the kill frame).
    • A two-beat low thump, one on the throw and one on the landing (low confidence: the narration overlaps).
    • Echoes: the punch-in plays only on elite, champion and boss kills, up to 6% (C2).

3.4 Lightning (v1; no Echoes skill uses it yet)

Build order, from structure to light. Sizes are in bw: the video's target is 143 px wide. The bolt built here is about 1.3 bw wide and 2.35 bw tall. The hit bolt is smaller (see "Size on a hit" below).

  1. The path. A thin, mid-value coloured line, never white.
  2. Exaggerate. Straight segments and hard angles. Push each of about 9 corners 0.04 to 0.08 bw further out along its turn, and add 1 or 2 kinks. With the straightening, the bolt grows about 16% wider. The push alone adds about 6%.
  3. Shape. Fat at every bend (about 0.1 bw against 0.02 bw on the runs, about 4.5 times). Pinch the runs thin, and make the tips needle-sharp.
  4. Broad glow underneath. A deep, saturated version of the hue at low opacity. Half strength about 0.19 bw out from the body's edge, gone by about 0.4 bw.
  5. Narrow glow. A brighter, lighter version of the hue. Half strength about 0.06 bw out from the body's edge, gone by about 0.1 bw (a quarter of the broad glow's reach).
  6. Accents. 10 to 12 small arcs and sparks round the bolt. Each stroke swells fast, peaks at about 20% of its length, then thins to a point. Only the still, finished drawing has them. v1 fades them out before the bolt moves. Every flipbook and hit drawing is body, glow and core only.
  7. The zap. A hard white core, about 0.04 bw wide (0.06 bw at the bends), drawn last.

  • Its glow runs about 2 bw from the top down to the target's crown (2.4 bw on the tallest variant).
  • Its white core zigzags 0.7 to 0.85 bw from side to side. That is the core's span, not its stroke width.
  • Its last segment drops straight down into the centre of the target's crown.
  • The video is a side view, so the bolt stands upright on screen. Under Echoes' 52° camera (CAMERA.elevationDeg in src/core/constants.js), a world-vertical bolt shows only about 0.6 of its height.

What the game lacks most

Echoes already does much of what the videos teach:

  • a white flash, a thump on the hit frame, and directional sparks;
  • a sim knockback of the right size, and a hitstop on melee hits and kills;
  • a kill pop, a burst, and a decal that stays;
  • template heads with hot cores and trails, and tapered slash ribbons with a faded tail;
  • pitch jitter on every sound.

The gaps, ranked by how much they would change the feel:

  1. The target barely reacts. Enemies flash, then slide. There is no squash, no flinch, no hop or tilt in the push, and no landing squash (R2, R3, R4). This is the videos' loudest point ("you can't tell you hit it"), and it is render only.
  2. The freeze holds nothing on screen. The sim stops for 33 to 50 ms, but the rigs, ribbons, numbers and kill pop run straight through it. The freeze is about a third of the videos' length. Only a melee arc freezes on a plain hit; a shot, spell, zone or bite freezes only when it kills (F1).
  3. The killing blow is too close to any hit. Its death flare is already about twice a basic hit's, and it gets a small ring. But the flare is bright for only about 50 ms, against the videos' 5 frames. It has no throw, and its sound is only 1 dB louder (K1, K4, A6).
  4. Spells and shots lack their beats. Nothing swells in the hand. The trail stops dead. There is no whoosh on a spell and no boom on its impact. Every shot is identical (S1, S5, A2, A3, S4).
  5. Damage numbers rise straight up. They have no arc, and crits have no tag (N2, N3).
  6. The attacker's body is soft at the key frames. The weapon kick ramps instead of snapping, the follow-through is cut with no overshoot, and there is no recoil off the hit (B4, B5, B3). The Tank pilot already fixes the follow-through for the Tank.
  7. Magic hits are radial. They do not leave through the far side (P1). The kill pop is radial, as in v2. It lacks only v2's slight lean along the hit.
  8. The chests just open. There is no wobble, press or snap before the lid (O1 to O3).
  9. Trails are not traced from the blade. No slash pattern pans along them (T1, T5).
  10. There is no lightning and no hero ice. The recipes in §3 are ready for when a skill is named.
  11. There is no photosensitivity rule for any future full-screen effect.

6.1 What every model standard slice carries ([M] rules)

Paste this into every hero, boss, champion or foe model brief, beside Kesh's two animation templates (the 3-hit combo, and the technical self-check). All of it is visual only, so timing, damage and the goldens do not change.

  1. B1 Anticipation without delaying the press. The next hit's wind-up lives in the last hit's settle. From rest, use at most 1 to 2 frames of wind-back inside the release. A spell's gather plays after the press.
  2. B2 Fast strike, held contact. The strike takes 2 to 3 frames. The contact pose holds through the hitstop, and the rig clip pauses on hitstop.
    • B9 Lunge into the contact (melee). About 3 to 5 frames before contact, pull back about 0.35 to 0.4 bw. Lunge about 0.85 to 0.9 bw past rest and lean 20 to 25° toward the target. Hold a squash at contact (+19% wide, −9% tall), then snap back in 2 to 3 frames.
    • Keep the lean and the squash at full size. Keep the travel to a lunge step that passes the model standard's root-motion check, like the Tank pilot's slam (its foot steps in about 0.16 u). The body never moves.
  3. B3 Recoil off the hit. A rig-only lean and shove of 0.08 to 0.12 u in 2 frames, fading over about 8 frames. The body never moves.
    • A shot (a hero bolt or a machine foe's fire) also squashes the shooter. The first shot squashes it about 20% wider. Steady fire deepens this to about 38% wider and 10% shorter by the third shot, and the squash holds while the shots keep coming.
    • After the last shot the shape is back in about 100 ms (3 frames), with a small stretch overshoot (about 4% taller) 4 to 5 frames after the last shot. The shove eases back over about 0.45 s.
    • A spell throw keeps B5's stretch into the lunge, not this squash.
  4. B4 The weapon kicks on the press frame, then settles. There is no ramp.
  5. B5 Follow-through with overshoot. It settles into the next wind-up, or into idle with one small overshoot, never a plain blend.
  6. B6 The third hit is the biggest. A hop, stretch or lunge, plus a bigger VFX and sound, along Kesh's 3-hit template.
  7. B7 Spawn-in. The body grows from the floor in about 7 frames with about 10% overshoot.
  8. B8 Expose the hand and the weapon tip as named points, so the hand swell (S1) and the traced trail (T1) can anchor to them.
  9. R2 to R5 Hit reaction (foes, champions, bosses).
    • The squash is 10 to 14% wide and 12 to 13% short, then a 6 to 7% stretch, settled in 5 to 8 frames.
    • A heavy spell hit (v6) squashes 18 to 30% wide and 9 to 15% short, springs to 5 to 10% tall and about 0.88 wide, and rests by +10 to 13 frames, with the hold included.
    • The flinch is a mid-body bulge away from the hit on frame 2. The base stays anchored, and the body neither leans nor travels (R3).
    • The knockback adds a hop, a 7 to 12° backward tilt in the air, an air stretch and a 1-frame landing squash (R4).
    • Bosses take half the squash and half the flinch. They get no knockback overlay, since the sim never moves them.
    • Champions take the squash and flinch scaled down, like bosses. They keep the knockback overlay (hop, tilt, landing squash) at the size of their sim push, 10 to 15% of an ordinary foe's.
    • If the VFX slice has built the shared envelope (§6.2 a), the model only tunes it to its body. A legless or coiled body squashes along its spine.
  10. K2 to K4 Death motion. A short round swell before the pop. On a blade or fire kill the body burns, and the pop still ends it (K3). The body is thrown along the last hit before it pops. A boss model keeps its own death. If the VFX slice has built the shared swell and throw (§6.2 c), the model only tunes them to its body.
    • Hero fire here stays Blaze Gold and Blaze Core, not Flame orange. The ring is gold and fades with the flash. No flame is left on the floor after the dissolve.
    • The burn never plays on a Molten elite's own death.
  11. Colour check. Hero fire is gold, foe fire is Foe Crimson, and nothing is Ember, Bright Heal or Violet unless the meaning is the same. Shape comes before colour.

The chest rules O1 to O3 are [M] too, but no model standard slice builds a chest. The VFX slice builds them with the rest of the chest beat (§6.2 i).

6.2 A VFX and hit feedback slice ([V] rules)

All of this is render and audio only, so the nine goldens stay identical. It is built once for every hero, all 42 foe kinds, the 12 champions and the 17 bosses. It is big, so it is proposed as two PRs in order.

Part 1: the hit and the kill

  • a. The enemy hit reaction envelope (R2, R3, R4 overlay, R5). Built in the shared enemy and boss render layers, tick-based so it holds through hitstop like the flash.
  • b. The render hold on hitstop (F1's safe route, I3). Rig clips, the slash ribbons' sweep (their noise keeps running) and flare time pause for the event's ticks. Particles keep running.
  • c. The kill. The death flare holds bright for 5 frames. The ground ring stays brief, gone within about 0.25 s, so it never reads as a telegraph (K1). The kill gets a heavier sound, with its weight in a rumble after the hit (A6). The body is thrown along the last hit (K4). The pop starts with a round swell (K2). The kill burst leans slightly along the last hit (P1).
  • d. Numbers. Your own outgoing numbers get a ballistic arc that alternates sides, a big white pop that holds through the freeze and settles to Hearth Amber within 3 frames, crits at 1.55 times, and a "CRIT!" tag in all ten languages (N1 to N3).
    • Other players' numbers keep Bone at 0.7 times and 45% (N4). They take the arc with their own small pop, never amber.
    • Heals stay Bright Heal with "+" from their first frame, with no white pop.
    • Incoming numbers keep their Bruise Umber drop.
  • e. Contact. A bigger bloom at the contact (P3), held at full strength for 100 to 167 ms (L5). Magic hits leave through the far side (P1). The flash fades on spell hits (R1). The first impact of a tick bypasses the audio budget (I1, A1).

Part 2: the cast, the shot, the reward and the aftermath

  • f. Shots and spells. The head swells at the hand (S1), stretches along its travel (S3) and trails longer behind it (S2). The trail drains into the hit (S5). Each shot gets its own size, brightness and height (S4, render only). Sparks show where a shot lands (P4). The light follows the flight (S7).
  • g. Sound. A whoosh on spell casts (A2). Impact families: a boom for spells, a thunk for arrows, a cut for blades (A3), each with about ±6% pitch jitter (A5). A whiz while each shot flies, from 1 frame after the shot, along its path (A4). A ping off walls (A4). My pick: play the whiz only on your own seat's shots and on foe shots that pass near your hero, so a busy room does not hiss. This is my limit, not v4's.
  • h. The weapon kick on the press frame for every rig, in the shared driver (B4).
  • i. Chests (O1 to O5, §2.14's safe route).
    • The vault chest (a press, 1.4 s reveal) and the secret reliquary and cache (a press, 1.2 s reveal, SECRET_RULES.revealSec) get the whole beat. They wobble faster and faster from when the hero comes near, press and tremble, then snap open with a hop, a 4-frame render hold at its top and a zoom punch of about 2% (C2).
    • The lid is flung open, never slammed shut. The padlock flies off and stays on the floor. A rim line and a light sheet flash on the snap. Light comes from inside with slow turning rays, then stars pop and sparks rise. Each layer gets its own sound.
    • The end of the wobble, the squash, the snap and the hold fit inside the reveal. The light and the stars run on after it.
    • The champion chest has no press and no reveal: it opens by itself on the room clear. It may wobble from the champion's fall to the clear. It plays the snap, the light and the stars there, and needs a short UI-only hold before the draft, like the 1.5 s take hold (TAKE_HOLD_MS).
    • The Trapped Chest keeps its lure twitch, so it still reads apart from a safe chest. Its Take springs the ambush at once, and its lid opens on Ember sparks from the lock, with no light and no stars. A reward beat, if it gets one, plays when the chest pays after the fight (event_chest), inside the 1.5 s take hold.
  • j. Slash ribbons. Traced from the weapon tip where a rig exposes one, reaching nearly to the hilt. A code-made slash texture times a panning mask on the blade template's trail. The swing's start fades in on a concave ease-out ramp, opaque by 40 to 45% of the arc. A tip glint on the first strike frame: a 4-point white star for 1 frame, its core about 0.3 bw and its spikes about 1.5 bw tip to tip, then a few short streak shards round the tip. The shards take the slash's own colours (template gold, or the Swordsman's silver), never blue (§4.3) (T1 to T6).
  • k. Anime accents. Only the local anime frame of §2.13: the ink silhouette, the focus lines and the spike star inside a disc of about 3 bw in radius round the contact point, for 3 to 4 frames. The focus lines run in from its rim and leave the figures clear. It plays on combo finishers and on elite, champion and boss kills. The full-screen panel waits for Kesh (§6.3 item 8).
  • l. Light and line. The effect lights the target's near side as it nears (R6). Strokes are exaggerated and tapered (L2, L3). A flipbook primitive of 3 to 5 drawings, swapped every 2 ticks (L4).
  • m. Aftermath. A gold rim of 0.8 s or less on blade-template hits, and a taper on the two real burns (D1). Spatter in the body's matter colour, and arrows stuck in the floor for a few seconds (D2). Twinkles and a faint mist that hold the place for 1 to 2 s (D3).
  • n. Ranged hold. A 3-frame render hold on shot and spell hits (§6.3 item 2). It holds the target's squash, flash and number, and keeps the shot's head at the contact point. The burst replaces the head when the hold ends.
  • o. Camera punch (C2). An FOV channel in camfx with its own clamp, scaled by Screen shake and Effects. Off under Reduced.
    • Up to about 6% (about 2.3° of the 45° FOV) on elite, champion and boss kills.
    • About 2% on a chest snap (i).
    • The 1.5% on your own melee contacts and the 3% (about 1.2°) on heavy hits and on combo finishers that do not kill are ordinary hits, so they wait for Kesh with F2 (§6.3 item 4).

6.3 Rule changes only Kesh can name

These touch the sim (the goldens move), a written ruling or a look the game already has. Each has my recommendation.

  1. A longer hitstop: about 6 ticks on a melee hit and 10 on a kill, and 18 to 20 on a rare climax beat (X1), against 2 and 3 today. My pick: build the render hold first (§6.2 b) and judge it on the laptop, since the cloud runs at about 1 fps. Then decide.
  2. A hitstop on spell and shot hits. v3 and v9 have none. v4's shots have none either, and its kill stops only the camera and the gun for 1 frame. v6 holds 3 frames. My pick: not in the sim, where it would eat the budget in a ranged fight. A render-only hold of 3 frames gives v6's look with no sim pause (§6.2 n). It holds the target's squash, flash and number, and keeps the shot's head at the contact point. The burst replaces the head when the hold ends.
  3. The hero bounces off the hit (ruling A5). My pick: rig-only recoil (B3).
  4. Shake or a zoom pulse on ordinary hits (§9 #7). My pick: on your own melee connects only: a small render kick (F2) and a zoom pulse of about 1.5%, up to 3% on heavy hits and combo finishers (C2).
  5. A cast wind-up before the press (BUILD_BRIEF §6). My pick: no; carry it in the combo settle.
  6. A random spread on shots (BUILD_BRIEF §6). My pick: no; use render-only variety.
  7. A camera kick on Archer and Healer shots (a change to today's look). My pick: a tiny backward kick (0.015 u or less) on your own seat's shots only.
  8. Full-screen silhouette or invert frames, and a photosensitivity rule. My pick: no full-screen invert. The local anime frame (§2.13, §6.2 k) needs no call, on combo finishers and on elite, champion and boss kills. Allow one full-screen silhouette and burst panel on a boss or champion kill when no telegraph is live, and adopt the 3-flashes-a-second rule (§4.3).
  9. A real burn status after fire hits. My pick: a cosmetic gold rim only (D1).
  10. The colours for ice and lightning, when a skill is named. My pick: decide with the skill. Gold-led magic is the only free route today.
  11. A longer vault reveal for the chest wobble. My pick: keep 1.4 s.
  12. A zoom punch near v5's 10% (§22). My pick: no; 6% at most.
  13. A bigger knockback on the third hit (B6). The sim keeps no basic-hit count today, so this needs a new sim counter and moves the goldens. My pick: no; make it bigger visually.
  14. A longer or bigger shake on a big finish (§9 #7) than F3's safe route: a camfx kick of 0.2 s or less inside the 0.09 u clamp. Corrections to the earlier hand-over notes

Each entry below was checked in the code at 7999610, then rechecked at gauntlet e1ba618. e1ba618 changes none of the values below. It does edit some of the files they rest on: src/render/boss/index.js and src/render/critters/index.js now build the faceted model standard rigs for the Barrow Wyrm and the Tank (with the same flash paths), src/render/allies/index.js cues the rig on each basic swing and cast, and src/render/critters/common.js gained addInstancedInk.

  1. Hit flash. 3 ticks at 1.0 holds for rank-and-file foes, dummies, nests, the waystone, the Escort's pilgrim and barricades.
    • Champions flash at 0.4 and the Gale Herald at 0.25.
    • Bosses use a separate envelope: 5 ticks, a linear decay from a peak of 0.28, and an 8-tick refractory.
    • Party members flash crimson #FF4A4A, not white.
    • The kill pop is a separate beat: every non-boss foe's pop starts at 1.0, a champion's included.
  2. Hitstop.
    • combatAllowed() is defined in src/sim/run.js, not world.js. It is also true before any run starts, so harness scenes get hitstop.
    • The budget window covers 21 ticks, inclusive.
    • Only a melee-arc connect that does not kill gets the 2-tick hit pause. Every other shape gets none, not only bites: shots, novas, ground zones, direct hits, cones, lanes, rings and auras. A kill of any shape still pauses 3 ticks. Breakables are the one exception.
  3. Screenshake.
    • Every kill shakes 0.06 u for 0.12 s wherever it happens, with no distance check.
    • 25 boss beats run 0.18 s, not only the stomp and the quake: the Stag's quake landing and trample, plus 23 ctx.shake beats across the other 16 bosses (src/sim/bosses/).
    • Hazards and kegs shake too.
  4. Anticipation. ANTICIP is 0.06 s.
    • On the heroes it plays after the sim has already resolved the cast, and it delays only the visible landing. It is wired to novas, ground placements, a few big skills, the Tidecaller's waves and the Bellwright's hung bells, never to shots or swings.
    • Foes get the same anticipate() gather. On boss and champion wind-ups (boss_telegraph_start, champion_tell and the per-boss wind-ups) it plays at the tell, before the blow lands. Some foe and boss moves gather and land 0.06 s later like the hero casts (the Thornling's plant, the Chime Wraith's toll, the Cindermoth's flare, several boss casts and the phase turns). The wasp dart and the kite dive do not wait.
    • It also plays on the key, vault, shrine, secret, tonic and relic beats.
  5. Impact.
    • The shockwave ring plays on crits only.
    • The light pool plays only for the Archer, Healer, Tidecaller and Bellwright.
    • The Duskwing gets needle stars. The Tidecaller and the Bellwright get round bursts.
  6. Ground marks. Hero class marks last 1.8 to 3.2 s. Across the VFX files, kit.mark runs from 0.8 to 8.5 s (the longest is the Hang Bell's floor ring: its 8 tolls plus 0.5 s).
  7. Randomness. The sim half of the rule holds. In the render, decals.js (the splat canvas), tonicfx.js (the fire-patch smoke and light flicker) and two rig fallbacks (the pose driver in critters/driver.js and the fall side in critters/index.js) call Math.random directly. Every caller passes a cosmetic stream today, so the two fallbacks never run. The whole audio layer calls Math.random too. The cosmetic stream is itself unseeded, so all of it is cosmetic and the goldens are safe.
  8. Crowd dimming applies only to the circle, beam, blaze and blast pools. Stardust, bits, sparks and heads are never dimmed.
  9. Effects: Reduced halves particles, sets camera kicks to 0, and dims light pools to 0.45 (it does not remove them). It does not dim kit.flash sprites.
  10. Screen shake has three steps (0, 0.5 and 1). It scales the sim shake's render amplitude and every camfx kick and dolly.
  11. Damage numbers.
    • Incoming numbers drop straight down. Their only sideways offset is the fixed fan slot set at spawn. The "sideways shake" exists only in two code comments (src/render/numbers.js, src/scenes/graybox.js) and in §17.
    • At the cap of 12, a number is released at once, not faded.
  12. Ember is not only on floor telegraphs. The foe matter layer still draws Ember sparks when an enemy's hit lands on the party, Ember motes on enemy wind-ups, and Ember on some landing beats.
  13. Ruling A5. Only §9 knockback skips the party. The sim still moves heroes in many other ways:
    • Bosses: Skyhorn's Horn Rush, Ash Raven's Wing Gust, Hourwarden's Hand Sweep, Cinderback's Tail Lash, Mire King's Tongue Lash and Swallow, and Veinweaver's Bind.
    • Champions: Gale Call, Antler Toss, Chain Hook, Rope Swing and Grate Blast.
    • Foes: the Vine Lasher's yank and the Rope Hanger's swing.
    • The Gusting elite, the millrace and gust vent hazards, and authored dashes and vaults.
  14. "Effects never hide a character" is softened by tall light shafts.
    • design-VFX §11's template beams: magic cast beams are 3.6 u tall, nova edges 2.4 u, placed-spell beams 5.5 u, and a champion's fall or a boss's death 5 to 6 u.
    • The kit's own pillars, which predate design-VFX §11, go higher: the vault key 7 to 8 u, the vault opening 8 to 9 u, and several secret, shrine and relic beats 6 to 7.5 u.
    • Both fade over their whole height, not above 1.6 u.
  15. Signal Blue is also the Rare colour, the slow ring and the glow on several relics. It also draws the Rootsnare rim and its tick rings, the Snare node's pulse, the Hunt's quarry mark and banner bar, and the Wishing Well's accent. It tints the scene's sky light, and many foe rigs mix it into their colours.
  16. The 20-telegraph cap is written in BUILD_BRIEF §1 but not enforced in code. The only limit is the governor: 2 player-targeted telegraphs at once, 72 ticks apart.
