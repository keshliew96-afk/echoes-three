v7 = v6 (every decision Kesh made today) plus the round-4 and round-5 critic fixes. History: `STORYLINE-v1.md` … `STORYLINE-v6.md`,

Built on Kesh's 大方向: the 王庭 (royal court) holds a ritual to summon a god; no god answers; the
世界屏障 (world barrier) cracks open a 裂缝 (breach); an Old God 旧神 notices and looks through it;

## 1. The story

**Before.** Long ago the gods 众神 sang the world into being. Their song became the **Veil 天幕**, the
world barrier in the sky, and its warmth lit the **hearth-fires 炉火** of every land. Then the gods
went silent, and one by one the hearth-fires began to die.

**The court.** The **Bell Court 钟庭**, the ibex lords of the high Spire, ruled by the **Ibex King
羱羊王**, would not let the fires go out. Generations ago they took a casting of the oldest hearth bell,
the one in the forest that still rings the gods' true note (Wick's bell; Wick's line never knew).
**This year** the court's bellfounder finally made a bell from it, **far bigger and louder**: a song
that *commands* instead of *asks*. It was cast in the Ember Kiln: the **Summoning Bell 召神钟**. They
hung it on the **altar 祭坛** at the top of the Spire, and the High Cantor sang the **Calling 召神仪式**.

**The night it went wrong (nine nights before the prologue).** No god answered. The note struck the
Veil and **tore it**: the **Breach 裂缝** is a tear in the sky above the Spire. The ground split too, in
a **Y lying on its side**: its **meeting point** is under the Barrow (the Violet Heart); one arm climbs to the
altar, one drops to the Kiln's fire, and the tail runs out under the graves. The ground split a breath before the Watcher's look landed: the High Cantor and the
Summoning Bell fell together down the upper arm to the meeting of the cracks. The Cantor stayed there; the bell rolled
on down to the fire that made it, and there it still **drones** the Calling. **That drone widens the
tear a little every night.**

**The Old God.** Outside the Veil, **the Watcher 凝视者** heard the bell and looked in. **Stone is its
look itself:** everyone inside the altar ring, where the look landed, turned to stone mid-song. **The
violet light is only the edge of its look**, and it falls through the tear on every land, strongest
along the Y. Creatures in the light hear **the Hollow 虚声**, a hollow copy of the gods' song. They
repeat it (they *echo* it) and lose themselves. Everyone in the court outside the altar ring went mad, and the palace's carved statues began to move, like the bodies in the graves.

**The verses.** When the Veil tore, its song fell in pieces, **one verse per land**, into the strongest
creature or thing there: the great beast of a wild land, the court's own in a court place, or a great machine. The light caught
each one and bent its verse into the Hollow. These are the **wardens 守护者**. A freed verse goes back
into the true bell, and **only the true bell with its verses can sing the torn part of the Veil back.**
The court's bell commanded a god; the true bell asks for nothing. It sings the Veil whole.

**Who stays sane.** The Hollow takes the strongest in a night and the small over many nights. The true
bell's **hum**, or Wick's singing at the hearth, undoes it. So Quill, Bramble and the small folk were
only half-taken when they reached the hearth; the party is safe while it walks inside the hum.

**Why every run is a new try.** The true bell remembers everything it has heard: friends met, truths
told. When the party falls, it rings them back to the hearth: **an echo**. Each run, won or lost, is one echo
of the same journey, which always starts on the ninth night after the tear. Seal and Recast are two
echoes, and the bell keeps both; that is why a new run can start after a win.

**The clock.** The verses only clear the light; the tear still widens every night, and when the
Watcher's eye fits through, it comes in. So they cannot wait.

**The heroes.** Wick's hearth is the last fire still burning. Its small band picks up the true bell and
goes: free each land's warden, take back its verse, and close the tear at the top arm (the Spire) or the bottom arm (the Kiln). While the
bell is away, Wick holds the fire by singing.

## 3. Chapters

Each chapter has five beats: **arrival**, **what went wrong in this land**, **the hero**, **the verse**,
**the clue**. They describe the land, never one boss, because a run rolls one of three bosses. Lines
about a single boss go in that boss's own voice line (the Hollow Voice).

**Unlock (Kesh's pick):** reaching a map unlocks its chapter and its heroes. This is a rule change
(today a hero unlocks on clearing the map before; an old profile's clear of level N counts as reaching N+1); it waits for the freeze and replaces the settled
rule in CLAUDE.md. **Hero beats** play once, on first reaching the map; the hero then goes back to the
hearth and can be picked from the next run. Clue lines are spoken by the bell or by Quill, never by a
hero who may not be in the party.

### Prologue · The Hearth Song 炉火之歌 (Wick, at camp)
"The gods sang the world, and their song kept every fire warm. Then they went quiet, and the fires
began to die. Ours still burns, because our bell still rings true. Nine nights ago a great bell rang far
away, and the sky tore open. Something is looking in. Take the bell. Take your friends. While the bell
is gone I can hold the fire only by singing. I am old. Do not be long."

### Chapter I · The Elderwood 苍林 · verse **Root 根**
- **Arrival:** the night the violet light came, the owls stopped calling.
- **This land:** the wood's old lords looked up at the light and could not look away.
- **Heroes:** the four of the hearth (§5).
- **Clue:** Quill: "I saw a light at the top of the Spire." (He leaves out the rest.)
- **Verse:** Root. The light over the wood thins; the freed warden's pale spirit comes to camp.

### Chapter II · The Drowned Bay 沉湾 · verse **Water 水**
- **Arrival:** the tide has stood at high water since that night; the mill turns backwards.
- **This land:** the warden of the bay holds the tide in. Drowned court bargemen float in the shallows.
- **Hero:** **Tidecaller (Rill)**, holding the last dry ledge with her shell. The light has half
  reached her; the bell's hum clears her head.
- **Clue:** at the ebb, a court barge in the mud holds a hymn sheet: "The Calling." Quill says it must be old.
- **Verse:** Water. The tide finally goes out, but violet water still runs down into the bay from the mountains.

### Chapter III · Snowridge 雪脊 · verse **Thunder 雷**
- **Arrival:** at the ebb the bay road climbs into the pass. Thunder rolls every few seconds; the storm never moves on.
- **This land:** the wind-tuners kept the storm-watch: weather vanes, giant tuning forks, signal horns
  that warn the valleys. The Calling's note rolled down from the Spire's peaks and the tuners went mad
  tuning their forks to the Hollow. No temples, no religion.
- **Heroes:** the **Snow leopard**, guard of the storm-watch, who could not stop the tuners; the
  **Alpaca**, a gentle shepherd of the high pastures whose flock turned violet. Both are half-taken; the
  bell's hum clears them.
- **Why not climb from here:** the stair from Snowridge to the Spire broke away that night. The only way
  up now is inside the crack, from the Heart.
- **Clue:** the wind-tuners' log: "the court's bell rang, and the sky tore." Quill is shown it and says nothing.
- **Verse:** Thunder. The storm finally moves on; the far side of the pass drops to the graves.

### Chapter IV · The Ashen Barrow 灰冢 · verse **Stone 石**
- **Arrival:** the grave bells ring the hollow note with no one pulling the ropes.
- **This land:** the tail of the Y runs under the court's royal graves. The Hollow moves the old kings'
  bodies and beasts. (Bodies moved by the song, not the dead themselves.)
- **Hero:** **Bellwright (Tolly)**, the last sane grave-bell keeper: she kept ringing her own small
  bell cast the old way, which rings the true note, so the Hollow could not reach her. Her teacher, the Gravebell Ringer, went mad.
- **Clue (on the clear card):** every grave bell wears the court's crest, and the same crest is stamped
  inside Quill's satchel. **Quill is caught.**
- **Verse:** Stone. The barrow floor splits open.

### Chapter V · The Violet Heart 空心 · verse **Heart 心**
- **Arrival:** under the Barrow, the caves glow violet and beat like a heart.
- **This land:** where the cracks meet. Something fell here from the altar: a singer's robe, a court crest in
  the crystal. Down here the light is strongest; whoever holds the Heart verse sings the Hollow back up the crack.
- **Clue:** a singer's court robe in the crystal; Quill knows it and confesses (below).
- **Hero:** **Duskwing (Sable)**, of the bat colonies of these caves. His colony and its matron went mad;
  he flew up, heard the bell's hum and followed it.
- **Verse:** Heart. The meeting of the cracks is cut; the light everywhere dims, but the tear is still open.
- **Quill confesses** at camp: the court sang the Calling to save the fires.
- **The two roads (on the clear card, whichever boss fell):** the bell hums two ways at once: **up** to the
  altar under the tear, and **down** to a drone in the Kiln.
  - **Spire:** close the tear where the eye looks in.
  - **Kiln:** silence the bell that keeps tearing it.
  (Defaults to the Spire after 30 s, as now.)

### Chapter VI-a · The Spire Court 王庭 · verse **Sky 天** · Ending **Seal 封印**
- **Arrival:** a spotless palace in silence. Inside the altar ring the Ibex King and his court stand in
  stone, mid-song, facing up. Beside the altar, the High Cantor's plinth is empty.
- **This land:** the court's servants outside the ring went mad. Over the altar hangs a silence that
  eats sound (in the Hush's runs it takes a shape: the hole in the song where the Watcher's look landed).
- **Ending Seal:** the party rings the true bell under the tear, with every verse they hold. The tear
  closes. **The eye closes slowly, and before it shuts it looks straight at the party: it will remember
  them.** No god answers. None is needed: the Veil was always the song itself. The fires warm again.
  The Sky verse muffles the Summoning Bell's drone far below: muffled, not ended.

### Chapter VI-b · The Ember Kiln 熔炉 · verse **Flame 火** · Ending **Recast 重铸**
- **Arrival:** the court's foundry; moulds of the Summoning Bell line the walls.
- **This land:** the moulds are still warm; copies are still being cast while the fallen bell drones.
- **Ending Recast:** the party breaks the Summoning Bell and recasts it with their verses into a bell
  that *asks*, like Wick's, and rings it in the Kiln **together with the true bell**. The crack that
  carried the drone up now carries the two notes up the arm to the tear. The tear closes. **The eye closes slowly and looks straight at
  the party before it shuts.** Far away, one hearth answers the new bell. The tear leaves a scar in the sky.

Seal: the door is shut, but the bad bell remains. Recast: the bad bell is gone, but the sky keeps a scar.
The seventh verse stays bent and its land stays violet: Seal leaves the Kiln, Recast leaves the Spire.

**Clue and verse in VI:** Seal: the clue is the court in stone (who froze and why); verse Sky. Recast:
the clue is the moulds (the founder never stopped); verse Flame.

### Endless, Daily, Boss Rush
- **Endless Descent:** an echo that does not end: the bell keeps ringing the party back, land after land.
- **Daily Descent:** one patrol a day.
- **Boss Rush:** Quill's chronicle of every warden, replayed.

## 3b. Why the party moves on 前进的理由

**The rule:** each verse **clears the light over its land** (and the land's fire warms again). Only at
an end of the Y can the tear be closed. Each verse freed makes the true bell **hum toward the next
land**, like a compass 罗盘. **The clock:** the tear widens every night.

**Quill's secret:** Quill was a court scribe. He fled the Spire that night and reached the hearth
half-dead on the eighth day. He knows the court sang the Calling and hides it out of shame. The log
(III) and the crest in his own satchel (IV) catch him; he confesses after the Heart (V).

| Step | Push 推 (why they can't stay) | Pull 拉 (why there) | What they learn |
|---|---|---|---|
| Camp → Elderwood | The light reaches the hearth; the wood's beasts attack. | The bell hums toward the wood's warden. | A great bell rang far away and the sky tore. |
| Elderwood → Bay | The forest river stops flowing; its water backs up into the wood, turning violet. | The bell hums downstream. | Quill: "I saw a light at the top of the Spire." (He leaves out the rest.) |
| Bay → Snowridge | The ebb shows the bay is still draining violet water down from the mountains. | At the ebb the bay road climbs into the pass; the bell hums toward the thunder. | A court barge in the mud holds a hymn sheet: "The Calling." Quill says it must be old. |
| Snowridge → Barrow | The pass will close with the first clean snow; the stair to the Spire is gone. | The far side of the pass drops to the royal graves; the grave bells ring the hollow note. | The wind-tuners' log names the court's bell. |
| Barrow → Heart | The barrow floor splits; violet light pours up from below. | The true bell and every grave bell hum toward the deep. | The crest. Quill is caught. |
| Heart → two roads | The meeting of the cracks is cut, but the tear is still widening. | The Heart verse hums two ways: the altar and the Kiln. | Quill confesses: the court wanted to save the fires. |
| Spire / Kiln | The eye is almost through. | The last verse: Sky or Flame. | Spire: who froze and why. Kiln: the founder never stopped. |

In the game this is text only: the clue and a line on each level-clear card, and a Quill line at camp per chapter.

## 4. The Old God 旧神: the Watcher 凝视者

- **What it is:** something outside the Veil, older than the gods. We see **an eye** behind the tear and
  **violet light**, and once, in the Heart, the shadow of something huge moving behind the eye. It does
  not hate the world. It noticed it, and it wants in.
- **Cause and symptom:** the **light** is the cause; **the Hollow** is what creatures hear in it.
- **Its voice:** the Hollow Voice (the violet line under each boss's title, in the game now) is the
  Watcher speaking through each warden.
- **The ending look:** at either ending the eye closes slowly and looks straight at the party first.
  No Eye boss is added now: the Spire keeps the Hush in its boss pool, and the Kiln gains its third (the Summoning Bell).
  An Eye boss is kept for later.
- **Seen in game (after the freeze):** the tear in each map's sky, a little wider each level.

## 10. 伏笔 Foreshadowing for DLC

**The idea:** the Summoning Bell rang once, and the world heard it five more times. **Each after-ring 余响 broke
something that is not the Veil:** the sea's floor, the ground's floor, the sky bridges, the mirrors, and
the door of the dead. After either ending the Veil is closed; these five are not. Ending card line:
*"The bell rang once. The world heard it five more times."*

Rules: a hint, never an answer; text, a prop or a far view; no new rules. ## 11. Snowridge 雪脊 (Level III, details)

- **Style:** snow passes, rope bridges, an old **storm-watch station of the wind-tuners** (weather vanes,
  giant tuning forks, signal horns that warn the valleys), frozen waterfalls, a storm that never moves
  on. Slate, snow white, brass, signal-flag blues and greens. No temples, no religion.
- **Verse:** Thunder 雷.
- **Bosses:** (1) **Snow Mammoth 雪猛犸**, storm-ice in its fur; (2) **The Storm Horn
  风暴号角**, the station's great signal horn, walking on iron legs and blasting thunder; (3, gated)
  **The Old Tuner 老调音师**, a takin 羚牛, head of the wind-tuners, gone mad, with a tuning-fork staff.
- **Champions:** **The Slide Breaker 雪崩破者** (ground slams that start snow slides), **The Thunder
  Drummer 雷鼓手** (drum shock rings).
- **Foes (6):** Storm Hawk 风鹰 (diver; the only bird), Crag Lizard 岩蜥 (climbs walls), Avalanche Pika
  雪崩鼠兔 (small swarm), Fork Sprite 音叉精 (floating support that hums buffs to foes), Ice Crawler 冰蜈蚣
  (burrows under snow), Rime Golem 霜傀 (freezes the floor).
- **NPC:** a **pass guide** who knows the roads (half-taken; keeps sane by humming the tuners' old signal tunes).
- **Followers:** Moth, Mantis, Weir Crab.
- **Heroes:** **Snow leopard** (spear guard 枪: lunges, sweeps, pole vaults; snow and wind) and
  **Alpaca** (gentle frost mage who breathes frost).

## 12. The bard 吟游诗人: Hymnscale 吟鳞 (hydra and gorgon blood 多首蛇 · 美杜莎血脉)

A wandering singer who fits every map, so no new land.

- **Why three heads (Kesh):** a bard must play and sing; with three heads she sings **three different
  songs at once**, one per head.
- **Look (for a top-down read):** a serpent with three heads, a bright frilled crest on each, gold eyes
  (gorgon blood: "stone-gaze blood" in player text), and a rattle-drum on her tail tip (no hands: the heads are the
  instrument, the tail keeps time).
- **Her need:** the night of the Calling, the light took the voice of her third head. Each verse the
  party frees gives part of it back, so she stays close to the party's road, one land ahead, to sing three songs again. Story only: in
  play she always sings three.
- **Gorgon blood:** her gaze turns things to stone, but only when a head's crest flares; she keeps her
  eyes hooded, so camp is safe. The light cannot enter through her eyes; her third head was singing with
  its eyes shut when the light fell, so the light took the open mouth's voice instead. At the Spire she
  sees the court in stone and understands her gaze is a small copy of the Watcher's; her line's first
  mother once looked out through a thin place in the Veil (a DLC hook).
- **Hydra blood:** a hurt head grows back.
- **Why everywhere:** she has walked every land collecting songs. Now she travels one land ahead of the
  party on the bell's old song-roads, which is why the party keeps meeting her in different lands.
- **Story job:** she sings the **DLC rumours**. Wick and Quill keep the main story.
- **Unlock:** her event room can appear on any map, and is sure to appear once per land until she has
  been met three times.
- **Kit direction (for her design thread, later):** three songs at once, one per head, each with its own
  effect and timer, all playing while she walks; the player picks which song each head sings, and the mix
  is the build. Petrify (slow, then stone, then shatter). Paths: **Hydra 多首** (heads regrow, many-head
  hits), **Gorgon 石瞳** (gaze, stone, shatter), **Chorus 合唱** (two or three heads singing the same kind
  of song makes it stronger). Bellwright keeps placed bells, so the two stay apart.
