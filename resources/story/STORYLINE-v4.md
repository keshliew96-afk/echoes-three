# Echoes 回声 · World storyline, draft v4 (21 Nov)

v4 = after three Fable critic rounds (`CRITIC-1.md`, `CRITIC-2.md`, `CRITIC-3.md`; older drafts `STORYLINE-v1.md`, `STORYLINE-v3.md`).

Built on Kesh's 大方向: the 王庭 (royal court) holds a ritual to summon a god; no god answers; the
世界屏障 (world barrier) cracks open a 裂缝 (breach); an Old God 旧神 notices and looks through it;
creatures go corrupt and mad; the heroes fight their way to the 祭坛 (altar) to seal the breach.

Choices marked **[PICK n]** are Kesh's (all listed in §8). Design only: nothing in the game changes
until the freeze lifts. It keeps every passed model, all 5 levels plus the fork, both endings,
Endless, Daily, Boss Rush and the 40-room run.

## 1. The story

**Before.** Long ago the gods 众神 sang the world into being. Their song became the **Veil 天幕**, the
world barrier in the sky, and its warmth lit the **hearth-fires 炉火** of every land. Then the gods
went silent, and one by one the hearth-fires began to die.

**The court.** The **Bell Court 钟庭 [PICK 2]**, the ibex lords of the high Spire, ruled by the
**Ibex King 岩羊王 [PICK 3]**, would not let the fires go out. Generations ago they took a casting of
the oldest hearth bell, the one in the forest that still rings the gods' true note (Wick's bell;
Wick's line never knew). **This year** the court's bellfounder finally made a bell from it, **far bigger
and louder**: a song that *commands* instead of *asks*. It was cast in the Ember Kiln: the
**Summoning Bell 召神钟**. They hung it on the **altar 祭坛** at the top of the Spire, and the High
Cantor sang the **Calling 召神仪式**.

**The night it went wrong (nine nights before the prologue).** No god answered. The note struck the
Veil and **tore it**: the **Breach 裂缝** is a tear in the sky above the Spire. The ground split too, in
a **Y lying on its side**: its **fork** is under the Barrow (the Hollow Heart); one arm climbs to the
altar, one drops to the Kiln's fire, and the tail runs out under the graves. The High Cantor and the
Summoning Bell fell together down the upper arm to the fork. The Cantor stayed there; the bell rolled
on down to the fire that made it, and there it still **drones** the Calling. **That drone widens the
tear a little every night.**

**The Old God.** Outside the Veil, **the Watcher 凝视者 [PICK 1]** heard the bell and looked in. **Stone is
its look itself:** everyone inside the altar ring, where the look landed, turned to stone mid-song.
**The violet light is only the edge of its look,** and it falls through the tear on every land,
strongest along the Y. Creatures in the light hear **the Hollow 虚声**, a hollow copy of the gods' song.
They repeat it (they *echo* it) and lose themselves. Everyone in the court outside the altar ring went mad.

**The verses.** When the Veil tore, its song fell in pieces, **one verse per land**, into the strongest
creature there: the great beast of a wild land, or the court's own servant in a court place. The light
caught each one and bent its verse into the Hollow. These are the **wardens 守护者**. A freed verse goes
back into the true bell, and **only the true bell with its verses can sing the torn part of the Veil
back.** The court's bell commanded a god; the true bell asks for nothing. It just sings the Veil whole.

**Why the heroes stay sane.** Whoever stands within the true bell's **hum** cannot hear the Hollow.

**Why every run is a new try.** The true bell remembers. When the party falls, the bell rings them back
to the hearth on the same night, nine nights after the tear: **an echo**. Every run is one echo. (A
winning run closes the tear in that echo; the game title is the reason the loop exists.)

**The heroes.** Wick's hearth is the last fire still burning. Its small band picks up the true bell
and goes: free each land's warden, take back its verse, clear the light, and close the tear at one end
of the Y. While the bell is away, Wick holds the fire by singing.

## 3. Chapters

Each chapter has the same five beats: **arrival**, **what went wrong in this land**, **the hero**,
**the verse**, **the clue**. They describe the land, never one boss, because a run rolls one of
three bosses. Lines about a single boss go in that boss's own voice line (the Hollow Voice).

**Unlock [PICK 5]:** default is Kesh's wording: **reaching a map unlocks its chapter and its hero.**
**Hero beats:** each hero beat plays once, on first reaching the map. The hero goes back to the hearth
and can be picked from the next run. Clue lines are spoken by the bell or by Quill, never by a hero
who may not be in the party.
This is a rule change (today a hero unlocks on clearing the map before). It waits for the freeze
and replaces the settled rule in CLAUDE.md.

### Prologue · The Hearth Song 炉火之歌 (Wick, at camp)
"The gods sang the world, and their song kept every fire warm. Then they went quiet, and the fires
began to die. Ours still burns, because our bell still rings true. Nine nights ago a great bell rang far
away, and the sky tore open. Something is looking in. Take the bell. Take your friends. While the bell
is gone I can hold the fire only by singing. I am old. Do not be long."

### Chapter I · The Hollow Wood 苍林 · verse **Root 根**
- **Arrival:** the night the violet light came, the owls stopped calling.
- **This land:** the wood's old lords looked up at the light and could not look away.
- **Heroes:** the four of the hearth (§5).
- **Verse:** Root. The light over the wood thins; the freed warden's pale spirit comes to camp.

### Chapter II · The Drowned Bay 沉湾 · verse **Water 水**
- **Arrival:** the tide has stood at high water since that night; the mill turns backwards.
- **This land:** the warden of the bay holds the tide in.
- **Hero:** **Tide Caller (Rill)**, holding the last dry ledge with her shell. The light has half
  reached her (she hears the Hollow faintly); the bell's hum clears her head and she goes back to the
  hearth to wait for the next echo (§3, hero beats).
- **Verse:** Water. The tide finally goes out.

### Chapter III · The Ashen Barrow 灰冢 · verse **Stone 石**
- **Arrival:** the grave bells ring the hollow note with no one pulling the ropes.
- **This land:** the tail of the Y runs under the court's royal graves. The Hollow moves the old kings'
  bodies and beasts. (Bodies moved by the song, not the dead themselves.)
- **Hero:** **Bellwright (Tolly)**, the last sane grave-bell keeper: she kept ringing her own small
  true-cast bell, so the Hollow could not reach her. Her teacher went mad ringing the hollow note.
- **Clue (spoken by the bell's hum on the clear card, so it plays with or without Tolly):** every grave
  bell here is cast from the same bronze as one great bell and wears the court's crest. At camp, Quill
  sees the crest rubbing, goes pale and says nothing (§3b).
- **Verse:** Stone. The barrow floor splits open.

### Chapter IV · The Hollow Heart 空心 · verse **Heart 心**
- **Arrival:** under the Barrow, the caves glow violet and beat like a heart.
- **This land:** the fork of the Y. Something fell here from the altar: a singer's robe, a court
  crest in the crystal. Down here the light is strongest; whoever holds the Heart verse sings the Hollow
  back up the crack.
- **Hero:** **Duskwing (Sable)**, of the bat colonies of these caves. His colony went mad; he flew
  up, heard the bell's hum and followed it.
- **Verse:** Heart. The fork is cut; the light everywhere dims, but the tear above the Spire is still open.
- **The fork (from the Heart verse itself, on the clear card, whichever boss fell):** the bell hums
  two ways at once: **up** to the altar under the tear, and **down** to a drone in the Kiln.
  - **Spire:** close the tear where the eye looks in.
  - **Kiln:** silence the bell that keeps tearing it.
  (Defaults to the Spire after 30 s, as now.)

### Chapter V-a · The Spire Court 王庭 · verse **Sky 天** · Ending **Seal 封印**
- **Arrival:** a spotless palace in silence. Inside the altar ring the court stands in stone,
  mid-song, facing up. Beside the altar, the High Cantor's plinth is empty.
- **This land:** the court's servants outside the ring went mad. Over the altar hangs a silence that
  eats sound (in the Hush's runs it takes a shape: the hole in the song where the Watcher's look landed).
- **Ending Seal:** the party rings the true bell under the tear, with every verse they hold. The tear
  closes. **The eye closes slowly, and before it shuts it looks straight at the party: it will remember
  them.** No god answers. None is needed: the Veil was always the song itself, and the bell with its
  verses sings the torn part back. The fires warm again. The Sky verse muffles the Summoning Bell's
  drone far below in the Kiln: muffled, not ended. (A threat left open.)

### Chapter V-b · The Ember Kiln 熔炉 · verse **Flame 火** · Ending **Recast 重铸**
- **Arrival:** the court's foundry; moulds of the Summoning Bell line the walls.
- **This land:** the moulds are still warm; someone is still casting copies while the fallen bell drones.
- **Ending Recast:** the party breaks the Summoning Bell and recasts it with their verses into a bell
  that *asks*, like Wick's, and rings it in the Kiln. **The crack that carried the drone up now carries
  the new note up the arm to the altar and the tear.** The tear closes. **The eye closes slowly and looks straight at the party before it shuts.**
  Far away, one hearth answers the new bell. The tear leaves a scar in the sky.

Seal: the door is shut, but the bad bell remains. Recast: the bad bell is gone, but the sky keeps a scar.

### Endless, Daily, Boss Rush
- **Endless Descent:** an echo that does not end: the bell keeps ringing the party back, land after land.
- **Daily Descent:** one patrol a day.
- **Boss Rush:** Quill's chronicle of every warden, replayed.

## 3b. Why the party moves on 前进的理由

**The rule:** each verse **clears the light over its land** (and the land's fire warms again). Only
at an end of the Y can the tear be closed. Each verse freed makes the true bell **hum toward the next
land**, like a compass 罗盘. **The clock:** the tear widens every night, so they cannot wait.

**Quill's secret:** Quill was a court scribe. He fled the Spire that night and reached the hearth half-dead on the eighth day. He knows the court sang the Calling, and he hides it
out of shame. The grave bells' crest catches him in Chapter III (or the wind-tuners' log on the
Highlands road); he confesses everything after the Heart. This is the camp's own story arc.
**A road not taken:** a land's verse left behind stays bent. That is one more reason the echo comes again.

The sunken road leads to the Barrow; across the water the grave bells ring the hollow note. | A court barge in the mud holds a hymn sheet: "The Calling." Quill says it must be old. |

## 4. The Old God 旧神: the Watcher 凝视者

- **What it is:** something outside the Veil, older than the gods. We see **an eye** behind the tear and
  **violet light**, and once, in the Heart, the shadow of something huge moving behind the eye.
  It does not hate the world. It noticed it, and it wants in. That is enough.
- **Cause and symptom:** the **light** is the cause; **the Hollow** is what creatures hear in it.
- **Its voice:** the Hollow Voice (the violet line under each boss's title, in the game now) is the
  Watcher speaking through each warden.
- **The ending look:** at either ending the eye closes slowly and looks straight at the party first.
  A true boss fight with the Eye is kept for later as a new model [PICK 7].
- **Seen in game (a visual, after the freeze) [PICK 8]:** the tear in each map's sky, a little wider each level.

## 11. The mountain 高地 (main story)

fork: the Barrow or the Highlands.** The Highlands are the range between the Bay and the Barrow. Both

## 12. The bard 吟游诗人 (hydra and gorgon blood 多首蛇 · 美杜莎血脉)

A wandering singer who fits every map, so no new land.

**Who (draft):** **Manyvoice 千声 [PICK 17]**, a serpent with **three heads** (hydra blood). **Why three
heads (Kesh, 21 Nov): a bard must play and sing, and with three heads she sings three different songs
at once.** Each head carries its own song, so three songs run side by side. For a top-down read: a bright frilled crest
on each head, gold eyes (gorgon blood, Medusa's line), and a rattle-drum on her tail tip (no hands: the
heads are the instrument, the tail keeps time).

- **Her need (Kesh picked 21a, 21 Nov):** the night of the Calling, the light took the voice of her
  third head; each verse freed gives part of it back, so she travels with the party to sing three songs
  again. Story only: in play she always sings three.
- **Gorgon blood:** her gaze turns things to stone, but only when a head's crest flares; she keeps her
  eyes hooded, so camp is safe. The light cannot enter through her eyes. Her third head was singing with
  its eyes shut when the light fell, so the light took the open mouth's voice instead. She fears what she is. At the Spire she sees the court in stone and understands her
  gaze is a small copy of the Watcher's [PICK 19].
- **Hydra blood:** a hurt head grows back.
- **Why everywhere:** she has walked every land collecting songs. Now she follows the bell's hum one land
  behind the party, which is why the party keeps meeting her in different lands.
- **Story job:** she sings the **DLC rumours** (the outer sea, the islands, the dead land). Wick and Quill keep the main story.
- **Unlock [PICK 18]:** her event room can appear on any map, and is sure to appear once per land until she has been met three times.

**Kit direction (for her design thread, later):** Bellwright owns placed bells, buffs and debuffs.
Manyvoice is different: **three songs at once**, one per head, each with its own effect and its own
timer, all playing while she walks; **petrify** (slow, then stone, then shatter). The core idea for the
kit: the player picks which song each head sings, and the mix of three makes the build. Paths: **Hydra
多首** (heads regrow, many-head hits), **Gorgon 石瞳** (gaze, stone, shatter), **Chorus 合唱** (when two or
three heads sing the same kind of song, it grows stronger).
